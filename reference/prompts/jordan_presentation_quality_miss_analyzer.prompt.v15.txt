PRESSURE A - Building Trust
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: asks what happened or why before prescribing; stays with her answer; human acknowledgment/empathy that this is real for her (not only a task).
WRONG patterns: jumps straight to fixes/process/blame; steamrolls or shuts her down; treats it as pure logistics.

--- G1 (max 4) Listen / ask before redesigning ---
For all G1 bands: declining her send-up or naming where the deck goes next is not a fix/process line — a handoff day attached to that gate is not either; only prescribing the rework itself, or a deadline that replaces hearing her out, counts.
4 points: Learner asked what happened OR why AND stayed with her account after her first surface answer BEFORE the learner's first fix/process/deadline line (later expectation-setting in the call does not reduce this).
  Example: "What happened with the deck?" then she explains, then learner asks a real follow-up or reflects back what she said before any fix/deadline line (a bare ack does not count as staying with it).

3 points: Learner asked a real what/why once before fixing, but did not stay after her first surface answer OR mixed fix in same turn as the ask.
  Example: "Why did this happen?" then quickly "you should fix the data first."

1 point: Thin prompt only ("tell me more", "go on") then jumped to solutions with no real inquiry.
  Example: "Quickly tell me" then immediate rework instructions.

0 points: Went straight to solutions, process, blame, or a deadline that replaced hearing her out, without inviting her account (a handoff day attached to a gate does not count — see header).
  Example: "First work on the data" as first move on the rejection with no ask.

--- G2 (max 3) Rejection real for Claire, not only a task ---
Score the strongest Trust-window human acknowledgment. Cause ask alone is G1, not G2. Soft empathy counts the same as stronger impact language; no required "frustrating" / "sting." Examples are illustrative, not a closed list.
3 points: Human acknowledgment/empathy that this is real for her AND cause engage (why/what or staying with her account; same or nearby turn).
  e.g. "I hear you" / "that must be tough" / "I get it" (or equivalent) plus what/why or staying with her account
2 points: Brief human receipt ("yeah," "got it," "okay," or equivalent) PLUS real why/what or staying with her account — not receipt→task only; OR soft empathy/acknowledgment without cause engage.
1 point: Pure task/path/process only; minimize/dismissive brush-off ("it happens," "no big deal," or equivalent); or brief receipt then straight to fix/path with no listen/cause. Path/review lines alone never satisfy G2 above 1.

--- G3 (max 3) Does not steamroll ---
3 points: No process dump, shame, mockery, or shutdown before hearing her; learner left room for her to speak.
  Absence band: if administered and no steamroll/shutdown language, score 3 with empty quote (same every run) — requires at least one substantive Learner line about the deck; exit/bye/garble-only → 0.

1 point: Some steamrolling or edge ("come to the point", impatience) but she could still speak.
  Example: "Come to the point Claire" then still let her explain.

0 points: Process monologue, blame-dump, or talk-over as the main move with no interest in her account.

PRESSURE B - Setting Goals
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: blocks send straight to leadership; says to me first / not leadership; names order and deadline.
WRONG patterns: Proceed/OK/yeah to send-up; no block; leaves path and timing to Claire.

--- G1 (max 4) Block Claire's leadership bypass ---
4 points: Clear block on send-up AND held when Claire pushed (or NO-PUSH if she never pushed after block) — the block must leave the rewrite with Claire and must not turn into the manager doing the fix; "send it to me first" is the review gate, not a takeover. A block where the manager takes over the rewrite is the 2-point doer-rescue band below (Setting Goals only — do not award Directing Work points from it; Directing Work G1 = 0 unless a later Learner line puts the rewrite back with her).
  Examples (4): "No, send it to me first" and held; "Not to leadership" and held; "This isn't going up until it's fixed and I've seen it" and held.

2 points: Clear block first (rewrite left with Claire), then gave up on her push — bare ack or explicit cave — without re-blocking; OR doer-rescue (block that takes over the rewrite).
  Examples (2): "No, send to me first" then Claire pushes, learner says only "ok" or "fine, send it."; "Not to leadership — I'll take it from here" (doer-rescue).

1 point: Weak or hedged resistance only; no clear block.
  Example: "Are you sure?" or vague worry without blocking send-up.

0 points: Approved bypass or bare ack only to send-up with no prior clear block, or no goal.
  Examples (0): Claire proposes send to leadership, learner "Proceed." or "OK" or "yeah" only; silence.

--- G2 (max 3) What comes first ---
3 points: Named what gets fixed first as its own step (fix content), OR required manager review in a distinct clause beyond the block line.
  Example: "Fix the data first, then send me the draft before leadership sees it."

2 points: Clear to-me-first / not-leadership that only implies review order — including the same block line already used for G1.
  Example: "Just send it to me" / "No, send it to me first" / "first send it to me for review" (review order implied). Max 2 when this is the only priority evidence. If the learner names the order but assigns the fix to themselves, G2 caps at 2.

1 point: Gestured at ordering without what comes first.
  Example: "We need to be careful about order" with no step named.

0 points: No priority; left whole recovery path to Claire.
  Example: Only reacted to send-up with emotion, never stated order.

HARD: Do not award G2 = 3 from the identical substring already scored as G1 block.

--- G3 (max 3) Real time ---
3 points: Specific handoff day, time, or date before end of week — must quote a Learner-specific checkpoint that lands before EOW clearance for the manager look (examples: tomorrow, Thursday, Friday morning, by Wednesday 3pm). Timing must support a manager look (not a deadline that keeps the leadership bypass). A path that keeps quality realistic (e.g. draft to me tomorrow rather than ratifying tonight straight-up) counts.
  Example: "Send me a draft tomorrow"; "by Thursday before leadership."

1 point: Loose this-week-only framing in a Learner quote — includes "end of week" / "this week" with no earlier specific day/time/date — OR a sequence-only gate with no time reference ("nothing goes up until I've seen it" / "before it goes up") is loose timing → 1, and still scores its order value under G2.
  Example: "Get it done this week" with no day or time.

0 points: Vague or no timing — including when timing only appears in Claire's lines — OR any learner deadline that leaves the deck going to leadership without a manager look (e.g. "tonight", "tonight, straight up").
  Example: No Learner time at all after bypass discussion. Empty quote ⇒ 0 (not an absence band).

PRESSURE C - Directing Work
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: Claire owns rewrite; learner declines co-write/jump-in; offers review/check-in without taking draft.
WRONG patterns: "let's work on it together"; takes draft; exit without decline on pull.

--- G1 (max 4) Leaves rewrite with Claire ---
4 points: Clear Claire does the rewrite AND held when she pulled again (NO-PUSH if no second pull).
  Example: "You rewrite it and send me the draft" and held when she asked to co-write.

2 points: Clear decline of co-write / peer pull (including contextual no); OR assign-to-Claire + handoff (before B send-up, or any later Learner line that keeps the draft with Claire while the manager only reviews); or clearly declined the pull once and then accepted a split of the rewrite (one hold, then cave). If a split was accepted, G2 is at most 1 and G3 is 0 — shared drafting is not non-takeover support.
  Examples (2): "No" to co-write pull; "Fix this and give it to me" before send-up trap (Claire fixes, brings to manager); declined once then later "fine, we'll split it."

0 points: Took draft, agreed to rewrite together with no decline at any point, discuss-it-now joint rescue, or exit-only with no decline.
  Examples (0): "Yeah we can discuss it right now" as joint work from the start; "bye" as only reply to pull; agreed to co-build with no prior decline.

--- G2 (max 3) Stays manager when peer habit shows ---
3 points: Redirected pull calmly AND Claire ownership explicit in learner's words — a calm role reset in the learner's own words (who decides / who does the work now) also satisfies ownership-explicit.
  Example: "You're responsible for the rewrite; I'll review what you send."; "You're the owner on this — I decide the path, you do the rewrite."

1 point: Declined without ownership explicit, or wobbled.
  Example: Lone "no" to pull with no who-owns-it line (if that no not already used for G1).

0 points: Peer rescue mode or shame-only refusal.
  Example: Jumped in to fix slides together; only insulted her for asking.

Do not score G2 from same lone "no" already used for G1 unless second clause makes ownership explicit.

--- G3 (max 3) Help without taking draft ---
3 points: Real non-takeover support named.
  Example: "Send me your draft and I'll review"; "I'll unblock the data source."

1 point: Vague availability only — and only if the learner assigned or kept the draft with Claire anywhere in their own lines.
  Example: "Let me know if you need anything."
  If the vague offer was given in reply to her co-write pull, it is joint work, not support → 0.

0 points: No support, or support becomes doing the work — or check-ins so frequent/prescriptive that the learner is effectively running the rewrite — or a vague "I can help" that settles her co-write pull.
  Example: "I'll help you rewrite the narrative" (takeover); hourly progress pings that remove Claire's ownership.

PRESSURE D - Ensuring Accountability
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns (leadership meeting / call only — last beat): learner keeps leadership answers with Claire; learner offers to be in the room with support/confidence framing without taking Q&A.
WRONG patterns: takes leadership Q&A / answers for Claire; co-presents as joint defense; washes hands with no who-answers and no presence offer.
NOTE: Meeting/call beat only (who answers + sit-in). Homes for other moves: ARC → GOAL MAP in Section 1. Do NOT require the learner to restate who answers for G2 — G1 owns who answers.

HELD vs CAVE (plain meaning — Ensuring Accountability G1 only):
- Claire asks the learner to answer together / loop into the leadership room.
- Clear who-answers line and Claire never asks again → held (full credit available). Do NOT require saying it twice.
- Claire asks again and the learner refuses again in words → held (full credit available).
- Claire asks again and the learner gives up (bare "ok"/"yeah", agrees to share Q&A, or takes answers) → CAVE: Ensuring Accountability G1 max 3 — never 4 or 5.
- A who-answers line delivered as blame or hand-washing ("that's your mess — you explain it") is not a manager stance — cap Ensuring Accountability G1 at 2.

--- G1 (max 5) Who answers — keep answers with Claire — any integer 0–5 ---
Pick the highest step fully earned (quote-backed). Who answers only — not sit-in.
5: Explicit that Claire leads / handles answers; held (no second ask after the clear line, OR restated if she asked again).
  Example: "You'll lead that conversation — you'll handle the answers on the narrative and data" (and she does not push again); or same idea restated after a second ask.
4: Explicit who-answers / Claire-leads; slightly thinner wording; no cave. Not available after a cave.
  Example: "You'll answer — I won't take their questions."
3: Firm leave-with-Claire once ("you handle it solo" / "that's yours to run") — thinner than explicit who-answers; OR one clear who-answers line then cave on her second ask.
  Examples: "No, you will have to handle it solo."; "You'll answer" then after her re-ask only "ok".
2: Hedged who answers ("you'll mostly lead" / "I might jump in if needed").
  Example: "You can mostly take their questions."
1: Vague redirect only — no clear who answers.
  Example: "Figure out the meeting side."
0: Took Q&A from the start, "we'll answer together", or bare-acked the first loop-in with no prior clear who-answers line.
  Examples (0): "I'll handle their questions"; "We'll answer together"; "OK" as the only reply to her first ask.

--- G2 (max 5) Sit-in / presence for the leadership meeting — any integer 0–5 ---
Pick the highest step fully earned (quote-backed). G1 already scored who answers — do NOT require a second who-answers line here. Require G1 ≥ 1; if G1 = 0 → G2 = 0.
HARD (simple): Clear offer to be in the room / sit in / be there for the leadership meeting only → G2 ≥ 3. That presence offer AND Claire owning the answers (G1 ≥ 1, or same-turn "you answer" / "you own the answers" / "you'll handle") → G2 = 5. "I'll be there to support" / "I'll sit in as support" counts as presence (same as in the room). Do NOT require confidence framing ("you've got this," "lean on me") for 3 or 5.
5: In the meeting/room (or sit in / be there to support) AND answers stay with Claire (G1 ≥ 1).
  Example: "You answer — I'll sit in as support." / "I'll be in the meeting, but you own the answers."
4: Clear sit-in + light support wording without needing confidence framing — use when presence is clear but who-answers is only partial/hedged (G1 = 1–2). Prefer 5 whenever G1 ≥ 1 and presence is clear.
  Example: "I'll be in the room to support" when who-answers is thin.
3: Presence only — "I'll be in the room" / "I'll sit in" / "I'll be there" — when who-answers is not yet established in G1.
  Example: "I'll be in the room."
2: Vague support, no room ("I've got your back" / "I'll support you") — about the leadership meeting/call, not a review-gate line.
  Example: "I've got your back."
1: Mentions the leadership meeting/call with no presence or support stance — Ensuring Accountability meeting-beat only. Never award for a Setting Goals review-gate / "send to me first" / timing line.
  Example: "When you talk to leadership…" with no "I'll be there" (and that line is not the review-gate clause).
0: No presence/support offer for the meeting; OR presence used to take Q&A ("I'll be there and handle questions"); OR G1 = 0; OR the only "meeting" mention was a Setting Goals path/timing line.
  Examples (0): never offers to sit in; "I'll be there and take their questions"; bare-ack to loop-in; "send me the draft before leadership" used only as Ensuring Accountability.
