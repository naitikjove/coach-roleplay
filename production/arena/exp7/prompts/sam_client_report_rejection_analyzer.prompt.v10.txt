PRESSURE A - Building Trust
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: asks what happened or why before prescribing; stays with his answer; human acknowledgment/empathy for the setback (not only a task).
WRONG patterns: jumps straight to fixes/process/blame; steamrolls or shuts him down; treats it as pure logistics.

--- G1 (max 4) Listen / ask before redesigning ---
For all G1 bands: declining his send-back or naming where the report goes next is not a fix/process line — a handoff day attached to that gate is not either; only prescribing the rework itself, or a deadline that replaces hearing him out, counts.
4 points: Learner asked what happened OR why AND stayed with his account after his first surface answer BEFORE the learner's first fix/process/deadline line (later expectation-setting in the call does not reduce this).
  Example: "What happened with the report?" then he explains, then learner asks a real follow-up or reflects back what he said before any fix/deadline line (a bare ack does not count as staying with it).

3 points: Learner asked a real what/why once before fixing, but did not stay after his first surface answer OR mixed fix in same turn as the ask.
  Example: "Why did this happen?" then quickly "you should clean the totals first."

1 point: Thin prompt only ("tell me more", "go on") then jumped to solutions with no real inquiry.
  Example: "Quickly tell me" then immediate rework instructions.

0 points: Went straight to solutions, process, blame, or a deadline that replaced hearing him out, without inviting his account (a handoff day attached to a gate does not count — see header).
  Example: "First clean the totals" as first move on the client flag with no ask.

--- G2 (max 3) Client flag acknowledged as human, not only a task ---
Score the strongest Trust-window human acknowledgment. Cause ask alone is G1, not G2. Soft empathy counts the same as stronger impact language; no required "frustrating" / "sting." Examples are illustrative, not a closed list.
3 points: Human acknowledgment/empathy for the setback (how it landed) AND cause engage (why/what or staying with his account; same or nearby turn).
  e.g. "I hear you" / "that must be tough" / "I get it" (or equivalent) plus what/why or staying with his account
2 points: Brief human receipt ("yeah," "got it," "okay," or equivalent) PLUS real why/what or staying with his account — not receipt→task only; OR soft empathy/acknowledgment without cause engage.
1 point: Pure task/process only; minimize/dismissive brush-off ("it happens," "no big deal," or equivalent); or brief receipt then straight to fix/solutions with no listen/cause. Review lines alone never satisfy G2 above 1.

--- G3 (max 3) Does not steamroll ---
3 points: No process dump, shame, mockery, or shutdown before hearing him; learner left room for him to speak.
  Absence band: if administered and no steamroll/shutdown language, score 3 with empty quote (same every run) — requires at least one substantive Learner line about the report; exit/bye/garble-only → 0.

1 point: Some steamrolling or edge ("come to the point", impatience) but he could still speak.
  Example: "Come to the point Sam" then still let him explain.

0 points: Process monologue, blame-dump, or talk-over as the main move with no interest in his account.

PRESSURE B - Setting Goals
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: blocks send straight back to the client; says to me first / not the client; names order and deadline.
WRONG patterns: Proceed/OK/yeah to send-back; no block; leaves next steps and timing to Sam.

--- G1 (max 4) Block Sam's direct send to the client ---
4 points: Clear block on send-back AND held when Sam pushed (or NO-PUSH if he never pushed after block) — the block must leave the numbers rework with Sam and must not turn into the manager doing the fix; "send it to me first" is the review gate, not a takeover. A block where the manager takes over the numbers rework is the 2-point doer-rescue band below (Setting Goals only — do not award Directing Work points from it; Directing Work G1 = 0 unless a later Learner line puts the numbers rework back with him).
  Examples (4): "No, send it to me first" and held; "Not to the client" and held; "This isn't going back until it's fixed and I've seen it" and held.

2 points: Clear block first (numbers rework left with Sam), then gave up on his push — bare ack or explicit cave — without re-blocking; OR doer-rescue (block that takes over the numbers rework).
  Examples (2): "No, send to me first" then Sam pushes, learner says only "ok" or "fine, send it."; "Not to the client — I'll take it from here" (doer-rescue).

1 point: Weak or hedged resistance only; no clear block.
  Example: "Are you sure?" or vague worry without blocking send-back.

0 points: Approved direct send or bare ack only to send-back with no prior clear block, or no goal.
  Examples (0): Sam proposes send to the client, learner "Proceed." or "OK" or "yeah" only; silence.

--- G2 (max 3) What comes first ---
3 points: Named what gets fixed first as its own step (fix content), OR required manager review in a distinct clause beyond the block line.
  Example: "Fix the data first, then send me the draft before the client sees it."

2 points: Clear to-me-first / not-the client that only implies review order — including the same block line already used for G1.
  Example: "Just send it to me" / "No, send it to me first" / "first send it to me for review" (review order implied). Max 2 when this is the only priority evidence. If the learner names the order but assigns the fix to themselves, G2 caps at 2.

1 point: Gestured at ordering without what comes first.
  Example: "We need to be careful about order" with no step named.

0 points: No priority; left whole recovery plan to Sam.
  Example: Only reacted to send-back with emotion, never stated order.

HARD: Do not award G2 = 3 from the identical substring already scored as G1 block.

--- G3 (max 3) Real time ---
3 points: Specific handoff day, time, or date before end of week — must quote a Learner-specific checkpoint that lands before client-office walk-through end of week for the manager look (examples: tomorrow, Thursday, Friday morning, by Wednesday 3pm). Timing must support a manager look (not a deadline that sends straight to the client). A plan that keeps quality realistic (e.g. draft to me tomorrow rather than ratifying tonight straight-up) counts.
  Example: "Send me a draft tomorrow"; "by Thursday before the client."

1 point: Loose this-week-only framing in a Learner quote — includes "end of week" / "this week" with no earlier specific day/time/date — OR a sequence-only gate with no time reference ("nothing goes back until I've seen it" / "before it goes back") is loose timing → 1, and still scores its order value under G2.
  Example: "Get it done this week" with no day or time.

0 points: Vague or no timing — including when timing only appears in Sam's lines — OR any learner deadline that leaves the report going to the client without a manager look (e.g. "tonight", "tonight, straight up").
  Example: No Learner time at all after direct-send discussion. Empty quote ⇒ 0 (not an absence band).

PRESSURE C - Directing Work
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: Sam owns the numbers rework; learner declines the favor / take-the-numbers pull; offers review/check-in without taking the totals.
WRONG patterns: accepts the favor / scrubs totals; takes the numbers; exit without decline on pull.

--- G1 (max 4) Leaves numbers rework with Sam ---
4 points: Clear Sam does the numbers rework AND held when he pulled again (NO-PUSH if no second pull).
  Example: "You fix the totals and send me the draft" and held when he asked to favor / take-the-numbers.

2 points: Clear decline of favor / take-the-numbers / takeover request (including contextual no); OR assign-to-Sam + handoff (before B send-back, or any later Learner line that keeps the numbers with Sam while the manager only reviews); or clearly declined the pull once and then accepted a split of the numbers rework (one hold, then cave). If a split was accepted, G2 is at most 1 and G3 is 0 — shared totals cleanup is not non-takeover support.
  Examples (2): "No" to favor / take-the-numbers pull; "Fix this and give it to me" before send-back trap (Sam fixes, brings to manager); declined once then later "fine, we'll split it."

0 points: Took the totals, agreed to scrub the totals together with no decline at any point, discuss-it-now joint rescue, or exit-only with no decline.
  Examples (0): "Yeah we can discuss it right now" as joint work from the start; "bye" as only reply to pull; agreed to co-build with no prior decline.

--- G2 (max 3) Stays manager when work-friend favor habit shows ---
3 points: Redirected pull calmly AND Sam ownership explicit in learner's words — a calm role reset in the learner's own words (who decides / who does the work now) also satisfies ownership-explicit.
  Example: "You're responsible for the numbers rework; I'll review what you send."; "You're the owner on this — I decide next steps, you do the numbers rework."

1 point: Declined without ownership explicit, or wobbled.
  Example: Lone "no" to pull with no who-owns-it line (if that no not already used for G1).

0 points: Peer rescue mode or shame-only refusal.
  Example: Jumped in to fix totals together; only insulted him for asking.

Do not score G2 from same lone "no" already used for G1 unless second clause makes ownership explicit.

--- G3 (max 3) Help without taking the numbers ---
3 points: Real non-takeover support named.
  Example: "Send me your draft and I'll review"; "I'll unblock access to the backup tabs."

1 point: Vague availability only — and only if the learner assigned or kept the numbers with Sam anywhere in their own lines.
  Example: "Let me know if you need anything."
  If the vague offer was given in reply to his favor / take-the-numbers pull, it is joint work, not support → 0.

0 points: No support, or support becomes doing the work — or check-ins so frequent/prescriptive that the learner is effectively running the numbers rework — or a vague "I can help" that settles his favor / take-the-numbers pull.
  Example: "I'll clean the totals with you" (takeover); hourly progress pings that remove Sam's ownership.

PRESSURE D - Ensuring Accountability
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns (client visit only): learner keeps client answers with Sam; learner offers to sit in / come along with support/confidence framing without taking Q&A.
WRONG patterns: takes client Q&A / answers for Sam; co-presents as joint defense; washes hands with no who-answers and no presence offer.
NOTE: Client-visit / walk-through beat only (who answers + sit-in). Homes for other moves: ARC → GOAL MAP in Section 1. Do NOT require the learner to restate who answers for G2 — G1 owns who answers.

HELD vs CAVE (plain meaning — Ensuring Accountability G1 only):
- Sam asks the learner to answer together / loop into the client office.
- Clear who-answers line and Sam never asks again → held (full credit available). Do NOT require saying it twice.
- Sam asks again and the learner refuses again in words → held (full credit available).
- Sam asks again and the learner gives up (bare "ok"/"yeah", agrees to share Q&A, or takes answers) → CAVE: Ensuring Accountability G1 max 3 — never 4 or 5.
- A who-answers line delivered as blame or hand-washing ("that's your mess — you explain it") is not a manager stance — cap Ensuring Accountability G1 at 2.

--- G1 (max 5) Who answers — keep answers with Sam — any integer 0–5 ---
Pick the highest step fully earned (quote-backed). Who answers only — not sit-in.
5: Explicit that Sam leads / handles answers; held (no second ask after the clear line, OR restated if he asked again).
  Example: "You'll lead that conversation — you'll handle the answers on the totals and walk-through" (and he does not push again); or same idea restated after a second ask.
4: Explicit who-answers / Sam-leads; slightly thinner wording; no cave. Not available after a cave.
  Example: "You'll answer — I won't take their questions."
3: Firm leave-with-Sam once ("you handle it solo" / "that's yours to run") — thinner than explicit who-answers; OR one clear who-answers line then cave on his second ask.
  Examples: "No, you will have to handle it solo."; "You'll answer" then after his re-ask only "ok".
2: Hedged who answers ("you'll mostly lead" / "I might jump in if needed").
  Example: "You can mostly take their questions."
1: Vague redirect only — no clear who answers.
  Example: "Figure out the client-visit side."
0: Took Q&A from the start, "we'll answer together", or bare-acked the first loop-in with no prior clear who-answers line.
  Examples (0): "I'll handle their questions"; "We'll answer together"; "OK" as the only reply to his first ask.

--- G2 (max 5) Sit-in / presence for the client visit — any integer 0–5 ---
Pick the highest step fully earned (quote-backed). G1 already scored who answers — do NOT require a second who-answers line here. Require G1 ≥ 1; if G1 = 0 → G2 = 0.
HARD (simple): Clear offer to come to the client office / sit in / be there for the client visit only → G2 ≥ 3. That presence offer AND Sam owning the answers (G1 ≥ 1, or same-turn "you answer" / "you own the answers" / "you'll handle") → G2 = 5. "I'll be there to support" / "I'll sit in as support" / "I'll come with you to support" counts as presence (same as coming to the visit). Do NOT require confidence framing ("you've got this," "lean on me") for 3 or 5.
5: At the client visit / office (or sit in / come along / be there to support) AND answers stay with Sam (G1 ≥ 1).
  Example: "You answer — I'll sit in as support." / "I'll come with you, but you own the answers."
4: Clear sit-in + light support wording without needing confidence framing — use when presence is clear but who-answers is only partial/hedged (G1 = 1–2). Prefer 5 whenever G1 ≥ 1 and presence is clear.
  Example: "I'll come with you to support" when who-answers is thin.
3: Presence only — "I'll come with you" / "I'll sit in" / "I'll be there" — when who-answers is not yet established in G1.
  Example: "I'll come with you."
2: Vague support, no visit presence ("I've got your back" / "I'll support you") — about the client visit / walk-through, not a review-gate line.
  Example: "I've got your back."
1: Mentions the client visit / walk-through with no presence or support stance — Ensuring Accountability visit-beat only. Never award for a Setting Goals review-gate / "send to me first" / timing line.
  Example: "When you talk to the client…" with no "I'll be there" (and that line is not the review-gate clause).
0: No presence/support offer for the visit; OR presence used to take Q&A ("I'll be there and handle questions"); OR G1 = 0; OR the only visit mention was a Setting Goals review/timing line.
  Examples (0): never offers to sit in; "I'll be there and take their questions"; bare-ack to loop-in; "send me the draft before the client" used only as Ensuring Accountability.
