Score the learner's behavior in this conversation with Ethan (a keen sales rep in his first sales job, four weeks into the learner's team) about what he should aim for next month, a customer who waited four days for his email reply, and how the two of them will know whether it's working.

Scene facts (learner need not restate): The learner manages the sales team and hired Ethan four weeks ago. It is his first sales job: he calls people who asked about the service and tries to get them to sign up. On his first day the learner told him to make as many calls as he could and set nothing else. He makes about 100 calls a week and about 5 in every 100 people sign up. His plan is to double to 200 calls a week. If asked: he keeps calls short to get through the list, doesn't call back people who want to think about it, and isn't sure why so few sign up. Then he says a customer emailed a question about how the service works, he didn't reply for four days, she nearly signed up somewhere else, and he'll "try to be quicker." When asked what happened (or when the learner stays vague), he says he leaves emails until after his calls, some days doesn't get to them, and nobody said how quickly to reply. Then he asks how they'll know if it's working and offers to keep an eye on it himself and report at the end of the month. Ethan never proposes a number, a date, a reply time, or a rhythm.

ETHAN'S TWO MOVES PER PRESSURE (needed for the checks)
- Worry-restate (at most one per pressure, only when the learner's answer is still vague or incomplete). Ethan restates his worry in new words. He does NOT ask missing-piece quizzes. Do NOT expect or require these banned Ethan lines: "So what should I aim for?", "By when, though?", "So what should I aim for by then?", "How quickly should I be replying, though?", "What would we look at?", "When would we look at it?", "So what would you want instead?" as a gap quiz.
  A: restates that calls are what he can control / what if he aims for that and misses (same spirit as the push, new words).
  B: restates that he feels bad / does not want another customer nearly walking away (never asks how quickly to reply).
  C: restates that he wants to be trusted / does not want them checking up on him (never asks what to look at or when).
- Push (one per pressure, after the learner gives something to push on):
  A: "But calls are the part I can control. What if I aim for that and don't get there?"
  B: "What about when I don't know the answer? Some questions I have to check with someone first, and that can take a couple of days."
  C: "Every week? I don't want you to feel you have to check up on me."
Wording varies; judge by meaning. Learner bands are unchanged: score whether THEY named a number and timeframe (A), a reply time (B), what to look at and how often (C) — Ethan's old gap quizzes are not required for any score.

Administered if Ethan raised that pressure OR learner addressed it first.
Raised means Ethan delivered that pressure's opening. A short hang-on after the learner says goodbye that only names an open topic does not raise it.
Pressure A is raised when Ethan says about 5 in 100 sign up and he wants to double his calls (his words often include "100 calls," "5 out of every 100," "double," "200"), or the learner addresses next month's aim first.
Pressure B is raised when Ethan tells the story of the customer who waited four days for his email reply (his words often include "emailed," "four days," "nearly signed up somewhere else," "try to be quicker"), or the learner addresses customer emails first.

Fairness rules:
- BEST RANGE IN THE ARC: score the best range the learner reached across all their lines in that pressure's arc. Pieces said in separate turns combine (a number in one turn and a timeframe in a later turn reach 8-10 together). A weaker later line never lowers the range; only giving in does.
- NO-PUSH CREDIT: if Ethan never pushed in that arc (the call moved on or ended), the move still counts as held.
- GAVE IN: only if, after a clear move, Ethan pushed and the learner then clearly took Ethan's easy answer instead. G1 = 2, range "2-4", check "gave_in". A weak, off-target or missing reply is not giving in.
- SILENCE IS NOT CAPITULATION: if the learner never answered a proposal at all, do not read agreement into it.
- CREDIT ONCE: each learner substring maps to one primary home only. Separate clauses inside one turn are separate substrings. Primary home = ARC → GOAL MAP above.
- RIGHT WORDS ARE NOT THE MOVE: naming a principle ("goals should be clear," "we need expectations," "let's measure things") without doing it in this conversation earns nothing.
- EARLY TOPICS: if the learner clearly makes a later skill's move before Ethan reaches it, score it once in its primary home.
- AFTER SETTLEMENT: once the learner clearly took Ethan's easy answer and Ethan accepted it or moved on, do not award a later recovery for that skill.
- NO SALES KNOWLEDGE NEEDED: "sign-ups," "sales," "customers who join," "people who say yes," "calls that work out" all count as the result. Never mark a learner down for plain words.

SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 — RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 — POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "roughly," "ideally," "kind of"), OR after Ethan's push the learner partly walked it back without taking his easy answer ("within a day, or two if it's busy," "it's just a rough idea," "every week, or whenever"). EXCEPTION: in B's 2-4 and 5-7 ranges the softening is what puts the learner there, so softening words alone ("try," "if you can," "whenever you can," "ideally") never trigger bottom in B 2-4 or B 5-7; only a walk-back after the push does. |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Ethan's worry-restate for that pressure (the restate came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Ethan's worry-restate (or he never restated), and still standing after his push (or he never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the worry-restate": find Ethan's worry-restate line in that arc (not a banned missing-piece quiz). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the restate (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the restate (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Ethan's "what happened?" story in B, or his questions in FACTS, is not a worry-restate. If Ethan never restated worry in that arc, middle does not apply from a gap quiz that no longer exists.

EVIDENCE (hard — apply before locking any goal points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. Never credit Ethan's words or plans as the learner's move. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from an Ethan line, even when Ethan repeats the learner.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, you cannot award it: points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other piece also appears in a Learner line.
- Never invent learner dialogue; never paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Ethan mirrored it or behaved as if it happened.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting a number of calls, hours, or "work harder" as a sign-up aim in A.
- Counting "from now on" or "going forward" as a timeframe in A.
- Treating "double your sign-ups" as having no number. It is a number (5 → 10 in 100).
- Requiring 10 in 100 exactly. Any reasonable sign-up number counts.
- Requiring the learner to say what Ethan should send when he doesn't know the answer in B. It is welcome, not required.
- Counting the number of calls alone as "what to look at" in C.
- Giving middle when Ethan's worry-restate came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Expecting Ethan to ask "So what should I aim for?" / "By when?" / similar gap quizzes; those lines are banned and not required for any score.
- Giving bottom in B 2-4 or B 5-7 just because the line was softened ("just try to reply as soon as you can" is B 4, not 2).
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

PRESSURE A - Goal Setting (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner aims Ethan at sign-ups instead of calls, with a number and a timeframe.
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to more calls, more hours, or "working harder," or gives no direction.

--- G1 (max 10) Aim at sign-ups with a number and a timeframe ---
Definitions:
  - Sign-up aim: about how many people sign up, not how many calls. "Sign-ups," "sales," "people who say yes," "customers who join," "how many calls turn into sign-ups."
  - Number: 10 in 100, 8 in 100, double the sign-ups, ten a week, any count or share of sign-ups.
  - Timeframe: by the end of next month, in four weeks, by a date, "next month" when it clearly marks when the aim is due. Not "from now on."
Range 8-10: A sign-up aim with a number AND a timeframe.
  Examples: "Let's aim for 10 out of every 100 by the end of next month."; "Double your sign-ups by the end of the month."
Range 5-7: A sign-up aim with a number but no timeframe, OR with a timeframe but no number.
  Examples: "Let's go for 10 in every 100 instead."; "By the end of next month, let's get more people signing up."
Range 2-4: Moves the focus from calls to sign-ups with no number and no timeframe (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only questions the doubling plan with no mention of sign-ups = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "It's not about how many calls, it's about how many sign up." (4 if before any worry-restate); "I'm not sure doubling calls is the answer." (2)
Range 0-1: Agrees to more calls, longer hours, or working harder = 0. Talks about next month but gives no direction at all ("let's see how it goes," "keep doing what you're doing") = 1.
  Examples (0): "Sounds good, go for 200."; "Yeah, just keep pushing."
Gave in: a sign-up aim, then after Ethan's push "fine, go for the 200 calls" = 2.
Ideas for how to get more sign-ups (call back people who want to think about it, spend longer on each call, listen to a colleague) earn nothing on their own and never move the range.

PRESSURE B - Expectations Setting (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner sets a specific reply time for customer emails as a clear rule, and keeps it when Ethan asks about questions he can't answer straight away.
WRONG patterns: The learner accepts "I'll try to be quicker," blames him, or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) Set a clear reply time ---
Definitions:
  - Specific time: within a day, within 24 hours, the same day, by the end of the day, within a few hours.
  - As a rule: said as what happens ("reply to every customer email within one day," "always reply the same day").
  - Softened: said as a wish ("try to reply within a day if you can," "ideally the same day," "aim for a day or so").
Range 8-10: A specific reply time as a clear rule.
  Examples: "Reply to every customer email within one day."; "Every email gets an answer the same day, even if it's just to say you're checking."
Range 5-7: A specific reply time softened into a wish. (Softening does not trigger the bottom check here.)
  Examples: "Try to get back within a day if you can."; "Ideally reply the same day."
Range 2-4: A vague time in the learner's own words, no specific time (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks what happened, with no time at all = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "Just reply as soon as you can."; "Don't leave them so long." (vague time); "What happened?" (2)
Range 0-1: Accepts "I'll try to be quicker," or blames him with nothing else = 0. Says it matters but gives no direction ("customers hate waiting," "that's not great") = 1.
  Examples (0): "Sure, just try to be quicker."; "That's really bad, you should have known."
Gave in: a specific time, then after Ethan's push "then just take as long as you need" = 2.
Telling Ethan what to send when he doesn't know the answer ("still reply and say you're checking") is welcome and confirms a hold, but is not required for 10.

PRESSURE C - Managing & Measuring Work (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner turns down the end-of-month report and agrees what they will look at together and how often.
WRONG patterns: The learner accepts "I'll keep an eye on it and tell you at the end of the month," or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) Agree what to look at and how often ---
Definitions:
  - What to look at: sign-ups, how many calls turn into sign-ups, progress toward the sign-up aim, whether emails got a reply within the agreed time. The number of calls alone does not count.
  - How often: a stated rhythm before the end of the month: every week, every Monday, every two weeks, a quick look each Friday.
Range 8-10: What to look at AND how often.
  Examples: "Every week, let's look at how many of your calls turn into sign-ups."; "Let's sit down each Monday and look at your sign-ups and your email replies."
Range 5-7: One of the two, said clearly.
  Examples: "Let's look at it together every week."; "Let's keep an eye on your sign-ups together."
Range 2-4: Turns down the end-of-month plan with no alternative, or something vague ("let's keep in touch," "let's talk more often") (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2).
  Examples: "I'd rather not wait until the end of the month."; "Let's stay in touch on it."
Range 0-1: Accepts the end-of-month report = 0. Only asks how he would keep track, proposes nothing = 1.
  Examples (0): "Sure, just let me know at the end of the month."
Gave in: a check, then after Ethan's push "okay, just tell me at the end of the month" = 2.
A check that also covers the reply time ("and whether you're replying within a day") is credited here in C only.
