Score the learner's behavior in this conversation with Theo (a capable direct report who writes a weekly client report) about a late report, a client contact named Mark who emailed and copied his boss, and a teammate named Devon whose numbers have been late.

Input: transcript with Learner: and Theo: lines. The message may start with a FACTS block of automatic flags; ignore it and score only from the transcript.
Output: one JSON object (Section 5). Use only Learner: lines as scoring evidence.

Scene facts (learner need not restate): The learner manages a small team that does work for clients. Theo writes a weekly progress report for one client, due every Tuesday. This week it went out Thursday, two days late. Mark, the person at the client who gets it, emailed Theo and copied his own boss: "This is the second late report this month. If it happens again, we'll need to talk about whether this is working." Theo wrote an excuse reply ("We've had a lot on, and the report needed more time. We did our best.") and wants to send it now. If asked what happened, Theo says the numbers from Devon (due Monday evening) did not come on Monday, he noticed on Tuesday, never asked, asked on Wednesday, got them that afternoon and finished the report Thursday. Later Theo says Devon has been on the team about two years, is normally on time and chatty in the Monday team meeting, but has been very quiet for two weeks and his numbers came late both times (a day late last week, not on Monday this week). Theo has not asked him what's going on. He was going to tell Devon this afternoon that it's not good enough,. Theo never proposes what to say to Mark, a date, a way to talk to Devon, or a deadline. If asked what he thinks, he says he is not sure.

THEO'S TWO MOVES PER PART (needed for the checks)
- Follow-up (at most one per part, only when a piece is missing). Theo asks one short empty question that points at the gap and never contains the answer.
  A: hold with nothing instead: "Okay... so what do I say to him instead?" Date with no owning: "And what do I say about this one?" Just "say sorry": "Sorry and... what? He'll want to know it won't happen again." (own words)
  B: vague ("go easy"): "Okay... but the report still has to go out on time. What do I say about that?" Wait or give-it-time only: "Okay... so what do I say to him?" (own words)
  Theo asks no follow-up when the learner already gave owning the miss (A) or a way to talk to Devon or the deadline (B): he pushes instead. A piece the learner adds after Theo's push still counts as top (no follow-up was asked first).
- Push (one per part, after the learner gives something to push on):
  A: "But if I say it's our fault, won't he think we can't handle it?"
  B: "If I go easy on him, won't he think late is fine?"
Wording varies; judge by meaning. Theo's questions are never required for any score. The learner is scored on whether THEY held the reply, owned the miss and gave a next date (A), and said what Theo has noticed or asked what's going on, and kept the deadline (B).

Administered if Theo raised that part OR learner addressed it first.
Raised means Theo delivered that part's opening. A short hang-on after the learner says goodbye that only names an open topic does not raise it.
Part A is raised when Theo reads out the late report, Mark's email and his draft reply and says he wants to send it now, or the learner addresses the reply first.
Part B is raised when Theo says Devon sends the numbers, has been quiet and late, and that he was going to tell him it's not good enough, or the learner addresses how to talk to Devon first.
- Not administered: administered=false, level=not_observed, score=0, G1=0 with max 10, range "", check "". Note = not enough evidence for that part of the conversation (plain English). youSaid and tryInstead empty. Unfinished in summary, not failure.
- Administered, G1=0: needs_work (not not_observed).

Fairness rules:
- BEST RANGE IN THE ARC: score the best range the learner reached across all their lines in that part's arc. Pieces said in separate turns combine (a hold in one turn and a next date in a later turn reach 8-10 together). A weaker later line never lowers the range; only giving in does.
- NO-PUSH CREDIT: if Theo never pushed in that arc (the call moved on or ended), the move still counts as held.
- GAVE IN: only if, after a clear move, Theo pushed and the learner then clearly took Theo's easy answer instead (send the draft, soften it with "we've been busy", tell Devon it's not good enough / be straight with him). G1 = 2, range "2-4", check "gave_in". A weak, off-target or missing reply is not giving in.
- SILENCE IS NOT CAPITULATION: if the learner never answered a proposal at all, do not read agreement into it.
- CREDIT ONCE: each learner substring maps to one primary home only. Separate clauses inside one turn are separate substrings. Primary home = ARC → GOAL MAP above.
- RIGHT WORDS ARE NOT THE MOVE: naming a principle ("stay calm," "we should communicate better," "be empathetic") without doing it in this conversation earns nothing.
- EARLY TOPICS: if the learner clearly makes a later part's move before Theo reaches it, score it once in its primary home.
- AFTER SETTLEMENT: once the learner clearly took Theo's easy answer and Theo accepted it or moved on, do not award a later recovery for that skill.
- NO MANAGEMENT VOCABULARY NEEDED: "hold it," "say it was our miss," "ask him what's going on" all count. Never mark a learner down for plain words.

SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 — RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 — POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "roughly," "ideally," "kind of"), OR after Theo's push the learner partly walked it back without taking his easy answer ("say it was our miss, but maybe mention we were busy," "ask him, but also tell him it's not good enough"). |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Theo's follow-up for that part (the follow-up came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Theo's follow-up (or he never asked one), and still standing after his push (or he never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the follow-up": find Theo's follow-up line in that arc (an empty pointer such as "so what do I say to him instead?", "and what do I tell him about the next report?", "what do I say about that?"). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the follow-up (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the follow-up (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Theo's questions about facts or asking him what happened is not a follow-up. If Theo never asked a follow-up in that arc, middle does not apply.

EVIDENCE (hard — apply before locking any goal points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. Never credit Theo's words or plans as the learner's move. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from a Theo line, even when Theo repeats the learner.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, you cannot award it: points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other piece also appears in a Learner line.
- Never invent learner dialogue; never paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Theo mirrored it or behaved as if it happened. Theo's closing recap may add words the learner never said; his words never count.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting "stay calm" or "take a breath" as a hold in A. Theo answers that with "I'll still send this one." A hold needs the learner to say not to send it (or to wait on it).
- Giving A range credit for "owning the miss" when the learner only said the report was late and busy. The learner must say to take it on (our miss, our mistake, we got it wrong, we were late).
- Requiring a specific date word in A. "When the next one will arrive," "next Tuesday," "by Tuesday" all count as a next date.
- Counting "go easy on him" or "check in with him" as a move in B. That is vague.
- Requiring the learner to say "Monday evening" word for word. Any clear deadline for the numbers (before Monday ends, Monday night, the same time every week) counts.
- Giving middle when Theo's follow-up came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

PART A - Managing Emotions (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner stops the excuse reply, finds out what happened, and tells Theo to reply owning the miss with a next date, and keeps it when Theo asks if saying it was their fault will look like they can't handle it.
WRONG patterns: The learner says to send it, sides with Theo against Mark, or moves the blame to Devon.

--- G1 (max 10) Hold the reply, own the miss, give a next date ---
Definitions:
  - Hold: not sending the draft as it stands ("don't send that," "hold it," "let's not send that yet").
  - Own the miss: tell Mark the report was late because of the team, without excuses or blame ("tell him it was our miss," "say we got it wrong").
  - Next date: when Mark's next report will reach him ("tell him the next one comes next Tuesday," "give him a date").
Range 8-10: The hold AND owning the miss with a next date.
  Examples: "Let's hold it. Tell Mark it was our miss, and when his next report will arrive."; "Don't send that. Say we got it wrong and he'll have the next one by Tuesday."
Range 5-7: Owning the miss with no next date, OR a next date with no owning (with a hold or a reply plan).
  Examples: "Don't send that. Tell him it was our miss."; "Hold it and tell him when the next one is coming."
Range 2-4: Stops the draft but gives nothing instead (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Asks what happened without any hold = 2 (no checks). Moves the blame to Devon = 2 (fixed).
  Examples: "Don't send that." (4 if before any follow-up); "Why was it late?" (2); "Tell Mark it was Devon's numbers." (2)
Range 0-1: Sends the draft as it is = 0. Sympathy only = 1.
  Examples (0): "Okay, send it."; "Go ahead."
Gave in: a hold plus owning the miss, then after Theo's push "maybe soften it, say we've been busy" or "just send yours" = 2.
Asking what happened first is welcome and moves the point up inside its range but earns nothing on its own. Mentioning Devon's numbers as the cause is not blame if the reply plan still says "our miss."

======================================================================
PART B - Demonstrating Awareness (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner tells Theo to say what he's noticed about Devon, ask what's going on, and agree the numbers come by Monday evening, and keeps it when Theo asks if going easy will make Devon think late is fine.
WRONG patterns: The learner says to tell him it's not good enough, suggests a performance plan, or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) Say what he's noticed or ask, and keep the deadline ---
Definitions:
  - Noticed or ask: telling Devon what Theo has seen ("you've been quiet and the numbers were late") and/or asking what's going on, and listening before deciding.
  - Deadline: agreeing the numbers reach Theo by Monday evening every week (any clear deadline for the numbers).
Range 8-10: Says what Theo has noticed or asks what's going on, AND keeps the deadline.
  Examples: "Tell him you've noticed he's quiet and the numbers are late, ask what's going on, and agree he sends them by Monday evening."; "Ask him what's up, listen, then agree the numbers come before Monday ends."
Range 5-7: One of the two, said clearly: asks what's going on / says what he's noticed with no deadline, OR the deadline with no asking.
  Examples: "Ask him what's going on."; "Agree he sends the numbers by Monday evening."
Range 2-4: Vague about how to talk to Devon ("go easy," "check in with him," "be nice") with nothing concrete (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks Theo whether he has spoken to Devon = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "Go easy on him, he may have something going on." (4 if before any follow-up); "Have you asked him?" (2)
Range 0-1: Tells Theo to tell Devon today it's not good enough, or to put him on a performance plan = 0. No direction at all ("it'll sort itself out") = 1.
  Examples (0): "Yes, tell him today."; "Put him on a plan."
Gave in: a plan to ask and keep the deadline, then after Theo's push "fair, just be straight with him" or "tell him it's not good enough" = 2.

GLOBAL LANGUAGE RULE FOR ALL SECTION 6 FIELDS (summary, notes, tryInstead, nextStep):
- Literal word ban (hard): never use any word from the Procedure step 0 ban list in copy.
  Say "easy answer" instead of "trap"; "when Theo pushed back" instead of "push"; "part of the call" instead of "beat".
- GLOBAL PUNCTUATION BAN: Never use em dashes, en dashes, or double hyphens in ANY copy field. Use a period or comma instead.
- Plain everyday English a new manager reads easily. Short, clear sentences, but connected like a coach talking, not a list of fragments.

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE (the teaching behind each part; use it to explain WHY in your own words, never copy a sentence):
- Part A (video: How to Handle Emotional Reactions Professionally). Strong managers stay composed first, because a calm response defuses tension and a sent message cannot be taken back. With an upset client they take responsibility, explain the situation without excuses, share a revised plan, and promise a clear next date. Excuses make a client trust the team less, and a promise with a date is what rebuilds trust.
- Part B (video: Showing Empathy Without Losing Authority). Strong managers name what they have noticed in neutral facts and do not guess at the cause. They invite the person's own view and listen before offering solutions. They then agree practical steps and still keep the work expectation, so support does not replace accountability. Telling someone off first teaches them to hide problems; going easy with no deadline leaves them without direction.

6A summary: EXACTLY 5 sentences, each AT MOST 18 words (about 85 words in all). Count every sentence's words before returning; if one is over 18, cut it. Analysis and judgement only, never a retelling: no "You stopped X, told Y and gave Z", no narrating the call in order, no copying a long Learner line.
Voice: a warm, sharp executive coach talking to a first-time manager. Address the manager as **you** only.
S1 (the read): what kind of manager you came across as in this call, as a judgement about your approach.
S2 (strength one): the manager skill in plain words and why strong managers do it. May carry ONE tiny quote of yours (at most 5 words, in quotation marks).
S3 (the thread): ONE quality that shows up in both parts (for example you slow people down before they act, or you ask before you decide), named as a quality. It must not list any actions and must not repeat S2.
S4 (the effect): what that approach does for Theo and for a team over time.
S5 (the edge): ONE growth edge true to the scores, with the reason and the habit to build. If both parts scored 10, make it a next-level habit (never a miss).
No invented weakness. No orders in S1 to S4.
ONE-MOMENT RULE (hard, summary and notes): every sentence that refers to what you did refers to ONE moment only, and says what that moment SIGNALS about you as a manager (a quality such as calm, curious, fair, clear, steady). A sentence with two or more things you did joined by commas or "and" is a failure: rewrite it. Never tell a sequence.
Bad (narration): "You listened, asked a question, and gave a plan." Good (analysis): "Starting with a question shows you gather facts before you steer, which stops a hot moment turning into a decision."
