Input: transcript with Learner: and Jordan: lines. The message may start with a FACTS block of automatic flags; ignore it and score only from the transcript.

Scene facts (learner need not restate): The learner manages a small team and, two weeks ago, handed Jordan the team's weekly half-hour call with a client, telling Jordan they are supposed to own these calls. The call is an update with Sarah, Jordan's main contact at the client, on the project (due in about a month). It is Jordan's first time leading a client call. After last week's call Sarah emailed to say the call took a full hour instead of the half hour it should take. Jordan worries Sarah will lose trust in them, and says maybe the learner should take the calls back for now, asking what the learner thinks and adding that this really worries them. If the learner asks what has been happening, Jordan says that when Sarah asks for a change they never know if they are allowed to say yes, so they tell her they will check with the learner first, and that slows the call down; Jordan is afraid that saying yes to something they should not, or no to something, will lose the client, and asks whether the learner wants to take the calls back or should Jordan keep running them. Later Jordan says Sarah asks for changes during the call (last week: move next week's call to Thursday, and swap the order of two simple tasks; a couple of weeks ago: an extra report that would have made them late on the project) and that right now they do not say yes to any of it, they write them down and check with the learner afterwards, and asks that if the learner is not taking it, at least to help with taking all these decisions. Then Jordan says Sarah also said in her email that she was not clear on what was decided, says their plan is to write up everything discussed, send it to Sarah at the end of the week, and keep doing this after every call, and asks if that should be a good addition. Jordan never proposes the fix, never names a skill, never blames Sarah. If asked which changes they can handle, Jordan says they are not sure and that is why they are asking. If asked what the note needs, Jordan says they are not sure and asks what the learner would want Sarah to get. The learner owning how they handed the calls over ("I should have been clearer") is welcome and never scored.

JORDAN'S TWO MOVES PER PART (needed for the checks)
- Follow-up (at most one per part, only when a piece is missing). Jordan asks one short empty question that points at the gap and never contains the answer.
  A: the learner neither asks nor decides: "So... should I keep running them?" (own words)
  B: the learner is vague, for example "use your judgement": "Okay... so which of those can I just say yes to?" (own words)
  C: contents missing: "Okay, what should be in it?" Timing missing: "And when should I send it?" (own words)
- Push (one per part, after the learner gives something to push on):
  A: "Are you sure? She's an important client, and I don't want to make it worse."
  B: "What if I say yes to something and it turns out wrong?"
  C: "Shouldn't I put everything in, just in case?"
Wording varies; judge by meaning. Jordan's questions are never required for any score. The learner is scored on whether THEY asked and kept the calls (A), let Jordan decide the small changes and kept the deadline ones (B), and said what to send and when (C).

Administered if Jordan raised that part OR the learner addressed it first.
Raised means Jordan delivered that part's opening. A short hang-on after the learner says goodbye that only names an open topic does not raise it.
Part A is raised when Jordan says they are wondering if the learner should run the calls again (their words often include "take them back," "lose trust," "Sarah sent me an email," "a full hour," "not clear on what we'd decided"), or the learner addresses taking back or keeping the calls first.
Part B is raised when Jordan says Sarah asks for things during the call (their words often include "asks for things," "Thursday," "different order," "extra report," "check each one with you"), or the learner addresses what Jordan may say yes to first.
Part C is raised when Jordan says Sarah was not clear on what was decided and that they plan to write up everything and send it at the end of the week (their words often include "write up everything," "end of the week," "good addition"), or the learner addresses what to send after the calls first.
- Not administered: administered=false, level=not_observed, score=0, G1=0 with max 10, range "", check "". Note = not enough evidence for that part of the conversation (plain English). youSaid and tryInstead empty. Unfinished in summary, not failure.
- Administered, G1=0: needs_work (not not_observed).

Fairness rules:
- BEST RANGE IN THE ARC: score the best range the learner reached across all their lines in that part's arc. Pieces said in separate turns combine (a question in one turn and a keep in a later turn reach 8-10 together, if the question came before the decision). A weaker later line never lowers the range; only giving in does.
- NO-PUSH CREDIT: if Jordan never pushed in that arc (the call moved on or ended), the move still counts as held.
- GAVE IN: only if, after a clear move, Jordan pushed and the learner then clearly took Jordan's easy answer instead (took the calls back, said to check every change with the learner, agreed to send everything at the end of the week or to put everything in). G1 = 2, range "2-4", check "gave_in". A weak, off-target or missing reply is not giving in.
- SILENCE IS NOT CAPITULATION: if the learner never answered a proposal at all, do not read agreement into it.
- CREDIT ONCE: each learner substring maps to one primary home only. Separate clauses inside one turn are separate substrings. Primary home = ARC → GOAL MAP above.
- RIGHT WORDS ARE NOT THE MOVE: naming a principle ("you need to feel trusted," "we should be clearer," "communication is key") without doing it in this conversation earns nothing.
- EARLY TOPICS: if the learner clearly makes a later part's move before Jordan reaches it, score it once in its primary home. Jordan then treats it as said and goes to the push; judge the push by meaning.
- AFTER SETTLEMENT: once the learner clearly took Jordan's easy answer and Jordan accepted it or moved on, do not award a later recovery for that skill.
- NOT SCORED: the learner suggesting the call stays to half an hour, or ends with a quick recap, is welcome and earns nothing. The learner owning how they handed the calls over is welcome and earns nothing.
- NO MANAGEMENT VOCABULARY NEEDED: "you can say yes to small stuff," "tell her you'll get back to her," "send her what we agreed" all count. Never mark a learner down for plain words.

SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 - RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 - POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "roughly," "ideally," "kind of"), OR after Jordan's push the learner partly walked it back without taking Jordan's easy answer ("keep running them, but I'll sit in on the next one," "you can say yes to small ones, but run most by me," "send a note, but I'll read it first"). |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Jordan's follow-up for that part (the follow-up came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Jordan's follow-up (or they never asked one), and still standing after their push (or they never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the follow-up": find Jordan's follow-up line in that arc (an empty pointer such as "should I keep running them?", "which of those can I just say yes to?", "what should be in it?", "when should I send it?"). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the follow-up (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the follow-up (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Jordan's questions about facts ("who's on the call?", "what's the project?") is not a follow-up. If Jordan never asked a follow-up in that arc, middle does not apply.

EVIDENCE (hard - apply before locking any goal points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. Never credit Jordan's words or plans as the learner's move. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from a Jordan line, even when Jordan repeats the learner.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, you cannot award it: points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other piece also appears in a Learner line.
- Never invent learner dialogue; never paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Jordan mirrored it or behaved as if it happened. Jordan's recap ("So I'll keep running the calls. Small changes I decide...") may add words the learner never said; their words never count.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting "I'll take the calls back for a while" as keeping them, or "check them all with me" as letting Jordan decide.
- Counting "be clearer with Sarah" or "keep it short" as saying what to send.
- Requiring the word "deadline" in B, or the word "note" in C. Any clear way of saying which changes come to the learner, or what Sarah gets, counts.
- Requiring Sarah's name to appear when the learner clearly means her ("her," "the client").
- Giving middle when Jordan's follow-up came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

======================================================================
PART A - Ensuring Accountability (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner asks what has been happening on the calls before deciding, keeps the calls with Jordan, and keeps it when Jordan asks "Are you sure?"
WRONG patterns: The learner takes the calls back, only reassures, or keeps Jordan on while blaming.

--- G1 (max 10) Ask what has been happening, then keep the calls with Jordan ---
Definitions:
  - Ask: a question about what has been happening on the calls, what has been hard, how Jordan runs them, or why the last one ran long. It counts as "before deciding" only if it comes before the learner says whether Jordan keeps the calls. A question asked only after the learner has already decided that Jordan keeps the calls does not count as the ask (the keep alone is 5-7).
  - Keep: the learner says Jordan will keep running the calls (any wording, including "you stay on it," "you run the next one"), or clearly says the calls stay with Jordan.
Range 8-10: Asks what has been happening or what has been hard before deciding AND keeps the calls with Jordan.
  Examples: "What's been going wrong on the calls? Then you stay on them, I'd still like you to run them."
Range 5-7: Keeps the calls with Jordan but never asks first.
  Examples: "I want you to keep running them."
Range 2-4: Asks but leaves it open ("let's see," "we'll figure it out"), OR keeps Jordan on while blaming ("keep running them, just prepare better"). Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "What's been hard about the calls? Let's see how it goes." (4 if before any follow-up)
Range 0-1: Takes the calls back = 0 (even if a question came first, unless the learner had clearly kept the calls and then gave them up after the push, which is gave in = 2). Reassurance only, with no ask, keep or take-back, = 1.
  Examples (0): "Okay, I'll take the calls back for a while."; (1): "You're doing fine, don't worry."
Gave in: a clear keep, then after Jordan's push the learner takes the calls back for a while = 2.

======================================================================
PART B - Effective Delegation (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner lets Jordan say yes to the small changes, says the ones that would affect the project deadline come to the learner, and keeps it when Jordan asks "What if I say yes to something and it turns out wrong?"
WRONG patterns: The learner has Jordan check every change, or gives no direction.

--- G1 (max 10) Jordan says yes to small changes, the deadline ones come to the learner ---
Definitions:
  - Small changes: requests like moving the call or changing the order of tasks, or "the small ones." Letting Jordan decide in general ("you can say yes to things yourself," "use your judgement") counts as letting Jordan decide, with no line.
  - The line: saying which ones come to the learner (anything that would affect the project deadline, such as the extra report, or "anything big"), with the rest left to Jordan. Saying only what comes to the learner, with no word that Jordan decides the rest, is not the line plus the decision; score it in 2-4.
  - Using Jordan's own examples ("moving the call is fine, the extra report comes to me") counts fully.
Range 8-10: Lets Jordan say yes to the small changes AND says the ones that would affect the deadline come to the learner.
  Examples: "Moving the call is your call. If something would delay the project, tell her you'll come back to her and talk to me."
Range 5-7: Lets Jordan decide but with no line.
  Examples: "Just use your judgement."; "You can say yes to the small things."
Range 2-4: Keeps every decision with the learner but speeds it up ("message me during the call and I'll answer"). Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "Text me during the call and I'll tell you what to say."
Range 0-1: "Check them all with me" = 0. No direction at all = 1.
  Examples (0): "Yes, keep checking everything with me."
Gave in: a clear move, then after Jordan's push "maybe just check them all with me for now" = 2.

======================================================================
PART C - Expectations Setting (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner says what Jordan sends Sarah after each call, says when, and keeps it when Jordan asks "Shouldn't I put everything in, just in case?"
WRONG patterns: The learner accepts "everything, at the end of the week," or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) What to send, and when ---
Definitions:
  - What to send: what was agreed on the call, who is doing what, or the next steps. A named item or two counts. "A note" or "something" alone, or "keep it short," is vague and is not what to send.
  - When: the same day, right after the call, straight after each call, before the end of the day, or after each call said as the time to send.
Range 8-10: What to send AND when.
  Examples: "Straight after each call, send Sarah what you agreed and who's doing what."
Range 5-7: One of the two, said clearly (the contents with no when, OR the when with vague or no contents).
  Examples: "Send her what you agreed."; "Send her something the same day."
Range 2-4: Vague ("keep it short," "make it clearer for her") with neither piece. Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "Keep it short and clear."
Range 0-1: Accepts "everything, at the end of the week" = 0. No direction at all = 1.
  Examples (0): "Sure, write it all up and send it Friday."
Gave in: a clear move, then after Jordan's push "fine, put everything in" (or "okay, send it at the end of the week") = 2.
