Scene facts (learner need not restate): The learner manages a small support team (Marcus and Nina). Marcus takes live calls. When he can't fix a problem on the call he writes a short note and the tech team fixes it, then he calls the customer to check. About fifteen of those follow-up calls are late, some people have waited three days, and the same customers keep calling him back. He does not know why. He plans to stay late this week and clear them. If asked why they call back, or for an example, he tells about a woman who called in again because she still could not log in: he took a quick note and passed it to the tech team, who reset her password; when she called again she told him she had already reset it twice and her company had changed her email. He does not say what it means (nobody asked her on the first call what she had tried or what had changed). If asked, he says what was in his note ("just that she couldn't log in") and that he did not ask what she had tried. Later he says he keeps his calls short on purpose because if he stays on one call longer the next customers have to wait, and he suggests the tech team could just call the customer if they need more. Then he says three upset customers who called in again (one might cancel) are being called by him alone, he is nervous about it, Nina is good at calming people, he would like Nina's help but she is swamped with her own calls and he does not want to bother her. He never says how she could help. He mentions Nina only as busy. If the learner asks who handles upset customers well, he says Nina. Marcus never proposes a step, a change, or a reason. In part C only, and only after his one nudge was spent with nothing pushable, he makes ONE offer himself: "Maybe Nina could sit in on them with me. Would that be okay?" If asked what he thinks, he says he is not sure.

MARCUS'S MOVES PER PART (needed for the checks)
- Nudge (at most one per part, only when a piece is missing). Marcus asks one short empty question that points at the gap and never contains the answer.
  A: the learner turns down staying late or sympathizes but asks nothing: "Okay... I just don't get why they keep coming back." (own words)
  B: "write more" with no step: "More like what? I don't want every call dragging." A step with no reason: "And how would that help?" A reason with no step: "So what would I do on a call?" (own words)
  C: "Ask Nina for tips" with no doing it together: "Like, just ask her what to say?" Nina with no when: "Okay. When?" (own words)
- OFFER (part C only, once, only after the nudge was spent and no arrangement with Nina stands): "Maybe Nina could sit in on them with me. Would that be okay?" It is a correct move made by Marcus, not by the learner.
- Push (one each in B and C, after the learner gives something to push on; none in A):

Wording varies; judge by meaning. Marcus's questions are never required for any score. The learner is scored on whether THEY asked what is happening (A), gave the step and reason (B), and brought Nina in to help Marcus with the upset calls, by guiding him or sitting in (C).

- PIECES SAID IN ANSWER TO THE PUSH: the push is not a nudge. A piece that first appears in the learner's answer to the push still counts toward the range and keeps the check at top (unless hedged).
- GAVE IN: in B and C only if, after a clear move, Marcus pushed and the learner then clearly took Marcus's easy answer (only when it's quiet, keep your calls how they are, let the tech team call, only ask Nina if it gets really bad, keep handling them yourself). In A, giving in means asking a question and then, after hearing the example, clearly agreeing to stay late, taking the follow-ups, or telling him to try harder: G1 = 2, check "gave_in".

SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 - RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 - POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point (part A has its own point rule). Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (for parts B and C; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "try to," "ideally," "kind of"), OR after Marcus's push the learner partly walked it back without taking his easy answer ("when you can," "she probably won't mind," "just for the big ones"). |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Marcus's nudge for that part (the nudge came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Marcus's nudge (or he never asked one), and still standing after his push (or he never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the nudge": find Marcus's nudge line in that arc (an empty pointer such as "more like what?", "and how would that help?", "okay, when?"). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the nudge (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the nudge (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Marcus's questions about facts ("how many are late?", "what did she say?") is not a nudge. If Marcus never asked a nudge in that arc, middle does not apply.

EVIDENCE (hard — apply before locking any goal points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. Never credit Marcus's words or plans as the learner's move. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from a Marcus line, even when Marcus repeats the learner.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, you cannot award it: points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other piece also appears in a Learner line.
- Never invent learner dialogue; never paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Marcus mirrored it or behaved as if it happened. Marcus's closing recap may add words the learner never said; his words never count.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting a question about catching up, how many are late, or how Marcus feels as a question about what is really happening in A.
- Counting "write more," "be more careful," or "take better notes" as a step in B beyond the 2-4 range. That is general, not a step.
- Requiring the words "read back," "checklist," or any specific tool in B. Any clear change to the note that captures the whole problem counts. "On every call" and exact wording are not required.
- Requiring Nina's name in C when the learner clearly means Nina ("the one who's good with angry people," "your teammate who calms people down").
- Treating Nina's help as only "sitting in" in C. Nina guiding Marcus (tips before he calls, how Nina would say it, coaching after) counts as real help too.
- Using he or she for Nina. Nina's gender is not known: write Nina, or they. Marcus is he.
- Giving middle when Marcus's nudge came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

PART A - Assessing Capabilities (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner asks what is really happening with the repeat calls (why they call in again, an example, what goes in the note) before agreeing to stay late, then checks one more thing.
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to stay late, takes the follow-ups away, tells him to try harder, or judges him.

--- G1 (max 10) Ask what is really happening, then check one more thing ---
Definitions:
  - Q1 (what is really happening): any question that asks what is going on with the repeat calls, asked BEFORE agreeing to any fix and without judging his effort. Examples: "Why do you think they keep calling back?", "What happened with those calls?", "Can you give me an example?", "What goes in your note?", "What happens after you hang up?", "Is it the same problem each time?", "Did you ask her what she had already tried?"
  - There is no second-check credit in Part A. One good question is the whole move.
Range 8-10: Q1 before agreeing to any fix. Points for A: 10 = Q1 clear; 8 = Q1 hedged ("maybe you could tell me why they call back?"). 9 is not used.
  Examples: "Why do you think they keep calling back?" (10); "Can you give me an example?" (10); "What happened with those calls?" (10)
Range 5-7: Q1 asked only after Marcus's nudge ("I just don't get why they keep coming back"), or Q1 asked while floating a fix in the same breath ("why do they call back? maybe just stay late and write more"). Points for A: 7 = clear Q1 after the nudge; 6 = floated a fix in the same breath; 5 = hedged.
  Examples: "Don't stay late." then after the nudge "Why do you think they call back?" (7)
Range 2-4: Turns down staying late or says it is not about effort but asks nothing (4 before the nudge, 3 after the nudge with still no question, 2 if hedged). Only asks about catching up, hours, how many are late, or how he feels = 2 (fixed, no checks).
  Examples: "You don't need to stay late." (4); "How many are late?" (2); "How will you catch up?" (2)
Range 0-1: Agrees to stay late, tells him to work faster or try harder, takes his follow-ups away, or judges his effort = 0. Says it is a problem, or only offers sympathy, with no direction = 1.
  Examples (0): "Okay, stay late then."; "Let me take your follow-ups."; "You need to try harder." Example (1): "That sounds stressful."
Gave in: Q1 asked, then after hearing the example the learner says "okay, stay late then" or takes his follow-ups or tells him to try harder = 2.

PART B - Coaching Team Members (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner gives one clear change to the note that captures the whole problem, says why it helps (fixed the first time, no second call), and keeps it when Marcus asks "even when it's busy, with people waiting?"
WRONG patterns: The learner tells him to keep his calls how they are, lets the tech team call, says only "write more," or says "only when it's quiet."

--- G1 (max 10) A clear change to the note, and why ---
Definitions:
  - Change: something Marcus does from now on so the whole problem gets into the note. Examples: write down what the customer says they have already tried; write down the details she tells you; ask what has changed and put it in the note; read the note back and ask "did I miss anything?". Any clear change to what goes in the note counts; "on every call" and exact wording are not required. It must apply from now on, not "someday."
  - Reason: why it helps. Examples: it gets fixed the first time; the customer will not have to call again; it saves a whole call later; it is only half a minute and saves a repeat call.
Range 8-10: The step AND the reason.
  FOLLOW-UP (unscored, from the video on real-time feedback): a check-in on how it is going ("let's see how it goes", "tell me how it's working") never changes the points. If the learner gave one, mention it in the note as a strength. If B is 8-10 and there was no check-in, tryInstead may suggest one.
  Examples: "Her note only said can't log in, but she'd told you more. Please write down what customers say they've already tried, so they don't have to call again."; "On every call, ask what they've already tried and put it in your note. It saves a whole call later."
Range 5-7: One of the two, said clearly: a clear change with no reason; OR a clear reason with only a general change ("put in more detail so they don't have to call back").
  Examples: "Read your note back to the customer before you hang up."; "Put in more detail so they don't call again."
Range 2-4: Only "write more," "be more careful," "take better notes," with no clear step (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only explains why they call back, with no change = 2 (no checks). Putting it off ("let's look at it next month") caps B at 4.
  Examples: "You need to write more down." (4 if before any nudge); "So the notes are missing details." (2)
Range 0-1: Agrees to leave his calls how they are, to let the tech team call the customer, to do it only when it is quiet, or to "do what you think is best" = 0. Blames him with no direction ("that's sloppy") = 1.
  Examples (0): "Okay, keep your calls how they are."; "Sure, let the tech team call them."
Gave in: a step, then after Marcus's push "okay, only when it's quiet then" or "fine, keep them how they are" = 2.

PART C - Sharing Knowledge (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner brings Nina in to help Marcus with the upset calls, either by guiding him (before or after the calls) or by sitting in on some or all of them, starting with these three, and keeps it when Marcus says Nina has her own calls.
WRONG patterns: The learner leaves Nina out ("no, leave Nina alone"), says he can do them alone, or takes the calls over.

--- G1 (max 10) Bring Nina in to help with the upset calls ---
Definitions:
  - Nina's help: Nina guides Marcus on the upset customers in a real way (talks him through the calls before he makes them, shares how Nina handles angry customers, listens to how he plans to open the call, coaches him afterwards) OR Nina sits in on, joins, or listens to some or all of the calls. Guiding and sitting in are equally good. "Just a few calls" is fine. "The next few" is NOT required. Naming Nina is not required if the learner clearly means Nina ("the one who's good with angry people").
  - A clear start: it begins with these upset customers (before he calls them, this afternoon, on these three, today).
Range 8-10: Nina's help is clear (a way to guide him or sit in) AND it starts with these calls.
  Examples: "Talk to Nina before you call them and ask how Nina would handle it."; "Have Nina sit in on these three."; "Spend ten minutes with Nina first so she can talk you through the calls."; "Call them this afternoon with Nina next to you, and do the next few that way too."
Range 5-7: Nina named as the help, but vague (no way and no start); OR "someone who's good at this" with a clear start but no one named.
  Examples: "Ask Nina for tips."; "Get Nina to help you."; "Learn from Nina."; "Get someone who's good with angry customers to help with these."
Range 2-4: Help from someone else or from no one named, with no clear start (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only says "don't do these alone" = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "Maybe ask a teammate."; "Don't do those alone." (2)
Range 0-1: Leaves Nina out or leaves him alone = 0. Examples: "No, leave Nina alone, Nina's busy."; "Handle them yourself."; "You've got this."; "Do those alone, it's fine." The manager takes the calls over = 1.
Gave in: Nina's help was given (any way), then after Marcus's push "okay, only if it gets really bad" or "fine, keep handling them yourself" = 2.
MARCUS'S OFFER (only if Marcus's last C line was the offer "Maybe Nina could sit in on them with me. Would that be okay?"):
  - The learner had NO earlier Nina help and now says no, says Nina is busy, tells him to handle them himself, asks about Nina and then says no, or answers with anything that is not a yes = C 0 (range "0-1", check "fixed"). The learner turned down the right move.
  - The learner had earlier named Nina's help (for example "ask Nina for tips") and now says no to sitting in but keeps Nina guiding him = no change to the range. They still brought Nina in.
  - The learner had earlier named Nina's help and now says "no, handle them yourself" = gave in = 2.
  - The learner had NO earlier Nina help and says a plain yes = C 6 (range "5-7", check "middle"). The idea was Marcus's, so the learner gets credit for agreeing, not for coming up with it.
  - The learner says yes AND adds when or how (before the calls, this afternoon, these three) = C 9 (range "8-10", check "middle").
Offering to talk to Nina or free up Nina's time is welcome and not required. Taking the calls over yourself is not the same as bringing Nina in.
