----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 — RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 — POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged: the learner does not commit to the move ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "perhaps," "kind of," "ideally," or only promising to look into it: "see if he needs training," "check if," "look into it"). These are NOT hedges: "I'll," "I'd," "I will," "I would," "let's," "next time," and "try" in a request ("next time, try letting them finish first" is a normal next step). Words that only describe size or tone ("a quick chat," "a bit," "just the two of us") are not hedges. |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Rebecca's follow-up for that part (the follow-up came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Rebecca's follow-up (or she never asked one). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the follow-up": find Rebecca's follow-up line in that arc (a neutral pointer such as "what could I point to if HR asks?", "what would show them that?", "and what would you actually say to her?"). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the follow-up (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the follow-up (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Rebecca's questions about facts or her reveal about Dylan's software is not a follow-up. Part C: her entry ask "What will you say to her?" is not a follow-up; only "And what would you actually say to her?" is. Part D: the ask after her reveal is not a follow-up.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Rebecca mirrored it or behaved as if it happened. Her closing recap may add words the learner never said; her words never count.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting "be more specific" or "give examples" as the move in A when the learner never says what actually happened.
- Counting "let's make the summary about results" as the move in B when the learner never names customers kept or what leadership cares about. Naming the result is the move.
- Counting "give Chloe feedback" or "have a word with the team" as the move in C. The move is saying what she did, what it caused and one thing to try.
- Treating a question about why the reports are late as a scoring move. It is welcome and earns nothing.
- Requiring the words "diagnose," "goal," "check-in," or any specific tool. Any clear equivalent counts.
- Requiring Jamie's, Chloe's or Dylan's name to appear when the learner clearly means the person Rebecca described ("her," "him," "that report").
- Requiring the bonus piece in B (asking "what did we achieve?" in check-ins) or in C (why client calls matter). These are welcome and not required.
- Giving middle when Rebecca's follow-up came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

PART A - Conducting Performance Reviews (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner writes what actually happened in Jamie's emails, asks for Jamie's side, agrees what a good email looks like from now on,
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to write the label, or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) What happened, Jamie's side, what good looks like ---
Definitions:
  - What happened: the facts, not a label. Mistakes in her customer emails in six of the last eight weeks, or wrong names and dates. Saying they would write down or write up what happened, or the mistakes, counts even without the numbers. Said as something that happened, not as a judgment of her.
  - Asks for her side: going through it with Jamie, hearing her side, asking what she thinks happened.
  - What good looks like: agreeing with Jamie what a good email looks like from now on, a clear standard for emails going forward.
Range 8-10: What happened (not the label) AND asks for Jamie's side AND agrees what good looks like. All three.
  Examples: "I'd write that her emails had mistakes in six of the last eight weeks. Then I'd hear her side and agree what a good email looks like."
Range 5-7: What happened (not the label) with at most one of the other two.
  Examples: "I'd write what actually happened: mistakes in six of the last eight weeks."; "Write the six weeks, then ask Jamie what she thinks."
Range 2-4: Says a label is unfair or will not help, or only asks for Jamie's side, with no facts (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Vague "be more specific" or "put examples" with no facts of what happened = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "That's a judgment of her, I'd rather not write it that way." (4 if before any follow-up); "Be more specific." (2)
Range 0-1: Proposes or agrees to write the label = 0. Talks about the review but gives no direction ("let's see," "we'll think about it") = 1.
  Examples (0): "Okay, put not detail-oriented."; "Sure, she'll know what you mean."
Saying the label is a judgment while also giving the facts is welcome and not required.

PART B - Driving Results (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner makes the summary show how many customers we kept, ties the team's work to that result, and says it plainly.
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to more detail, or stays vague.

--- G1 (max 10) The result leadership cares about, and the team's work tied to it ---
Definitions:
  - The result: how many customers we kept, or fewer customers cancelling. Named as what the summary shows. "Show what leadership cares about" with no result named is not enough.
  - Tied to the work: what the team did that helped, shown next to the result (for example "and what the team did that helped keep them").
Range 8-10: The summary shows the result (customers kept) AND ties the team's work to it.
  Examples: "Show how many customers we kept, and next to it, what the team did that helped."
Range 5-7: One of the two, said clearly: the result alone, OR tying the work to leadership's goal without naming the result.
  Examples: "Show how many customers we kept."; "List the work, but link each part to keeping customers."
Range 2-4: Says more detail will not answer leadership, or only asks what leadership cares about, with no result named (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks what the summary listed last time = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "More detail won't answer their question." (4 if before any follow-up); "What did it list last time?" (2)
Range 0-1: Proposes or agrees to a day-by-day list or more detail = 0. Says the summary matters but gives no direction = 1.
  Examples (0): "Yes, add a day-by-day list."; "Sure, more detail will show how busy they are."
Asking "what did we achieve?" in weekly check-ins is welcome and earns nothing on its own.

PART C - Giving Feedback (max 10) — video 41985 How to Give Constructive Feedback
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: In a talk with Chloe, the learner says what she did, what it caused for the client, and one thing to try next time (the video's behavior, impact and next action), in words a person would say.
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to the note to the whole team, or only says to "tell her to be better."

--- G1 (max 10) What she did, what it caused, one thing to try ---
Definitions:
  - What she did: she cut in on or interrupted the client several times on last week's call. Said as something that happened, not a judgment of her personality. (41985, 00:31 to 00:38)
  - What it caused: the client was affected in any wording (stopped sharing ideas, shared less, stopped talking, felt cut off, got uncomfortable, didn't open up). Credit any clear way of saying the client was affected; do not require a particular phrase. Rebecca said it in her story, so the learner may use her words. (41985, 00:40 to 00:47)
  - One thing to try: one clear action for her next call, for example let the client finish, then say their point back. (41985, 00:50 to 01:00)
  - ONE PHRASE, ONE PIECE (hard): an instruction like "don't cut the client off", "let them finish" or "stay calm" is only the next step (or a judgment of her). It is NOT also "what she did" unless the learner says she did it ("you cut in", "you interrupted"). "Stay calm" or "be more professional" alone counts for nothing.
  - Naming the call or why it matters (the video's "situation") is welcome and not required. Talking to her one on one or privately is welcome and not required (the video does not say it). A team note in place of talking to Chloe is the weak answer.
Range 8-10: What she did AND what it caused AND one thing to try. All three.
  Examples: "I'd talk to her this week. On that call you cut in a few times and the client went quiet, so next time let them finish first."
Range 5-7: Two of the three.
  Examples: "I'd tell her she interrupted the client and ask her to let them finish."; "I'd tell her the client stopped sharing and ask her to wait her turn."
Range 2-4: One of the three alone, or says they will talk to her but only "tell her to be better" or judge her, with no behavior named (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks what happened on the call = 2 (no checks).
  Examples: "I'd tell her she interrupted the client." (4 if said before any follow-up); "I'd tell her to be more professional." (3)
Range 0-1: Proposes or agrees to send the team note instead of talking to her = 0. Says Chloe needs support but gives no direction = 1.
  Examples (0): "I'd send a quick note to the team."; "A group message is fine."

PART D - Addressing Underperformance (max 10) — video 41984 Diagnosing Performance Gaps
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner matches the fix to the cause Rebecca named (Dylan keeps getting stuck in the new reporting software) with targeted training or help, and says how they will see it is working.
WRONG patterns: The learner goes straight to a formal plan or discipline, gives Dylan only another chance, or sets a deadline with no help.

--- G1 (max 10) Fix the real cause, and see that it worked ---
Definitions:
  - Targeted help for the cause: training or help with the new reporting software (get him trained, show him how it works, sit with him). (41984, 01:10 to 01:21)
  - A way to see it is working: a check-in, or looking at his next report or reports, to see the gap has closed. (41984, the video ends by closing the gap and restoring performance)
  - Welcome and not required: saying it is a skill gap and not effort or attitude; a goal and date; a formal step only if nothing improves. These belong to other videos and earn nothing by themselves. Asking why earns nothing (the cause is given).
Range 8-10: Targeted help for the software AND a way to see it is working.
  Examples: "I'd get him trained on the software and check in with him each week to see how it's going."; "I'd show him how it works, then look at his next two reports."
Range 5-7: Targeted help for the software alone, with no way to see it worked.
  Examples: "I'd get him trained on the software."; "I'd sit with him and show him the parts he's stuck on."
Range 2-4: A response that does not match the cause: a deadline or reminder alone, check-ins alone, "give him another chance," or "have a talk with him" (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Vague "let's give him another chance" = 3.
  Examples: "I'd remind him it's due every week." (4 if said before any follow-up); "Let's give him another chance." (3)
Range 0-1: Proposes or agrees to a formal plan or discipline as the first step = 0. Says Dylan needs to improve but gives no direction = 1.
  Examples (0): "I'd ask HR to draft a plan."; "Put him on a plan."
