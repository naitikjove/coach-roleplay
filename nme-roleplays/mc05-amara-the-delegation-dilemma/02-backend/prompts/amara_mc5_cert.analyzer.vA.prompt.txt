Scene facts (learner need not restate): Amara plans the weekly work for the six-person team and the learner is the manager she brings decisions to. A new request has come in from the sales team: a short comparison, one or two pages, of how two competitors price their service, so sales can use it with customers. It is due at the end of the month, about three weeks away. Amara leans to Dev: he has been on the team the longest and is the quickest. Lena joined three months ago and Amara is not sure about her. Amara does not say this out of order; the facts about workload and past work come out only if the learner asks. If asked: Dev is already doing the monthly customer report and building the new onboarding checklist for new customers, and he has been working late. Lena has some bandwidth this month, did a comparison like this at her last job, and has asked Amara for something bigger to work on, but she is slower and Amara did not want to risk it. If the learner picks Lena, Amara pushes once: "Are you sure? If it comes back weak, the sales team will blame us, and Dev could do it in two days." Then Amara says Lena has not done work like this here, the sales team is waiting, and she is a bit nervous, so she thought Lena would send her a short update every day and Amara would look over each draft before Lena goes further, so problems get caught early, at a cost of time for both of them, and asks what the learner thinks. If the learner turns that down and sets one review, Amara pushes once: "But what if she's stuck and doesn't say anything until it's late?" Then Amara raises Dev: every month he puts together the report the team sends to customers; last month he stayed late twice to fix mistakes in it before it went out and called the customer early to say one number had changed, before they noticed; he did not mention any of it and nobody has said anything to him; he is quiet and does not like attention; Amara says it is part of his job anyway and plans to send him a quick private message saying thanks for his hard work, because doing more might make others feel left out. If the learner says they will tell Dev specifically and let the team hear it, Amara pushes once: "Are you sure? I really don't want him to feel embarrassed." Amara never proposes the fix, never names a skill, never blames anyone. The learner thanking Amara for checking with them is welcome and never scored.

AMARA'S TWO MOVES PER PART (needed for the checks)
- Follow-up (at most one per part, only when a piece is missing). Amara asks one short empty question that points at the gap and never contains the answer.
  A: the learner neither asks nor picks: "Okay... so who do you want to take it?" (own words). If the learner asks a question, Amara answers it with the facts above; answering a fact question is not a follow-up.
  B: the learner is vague, for example "keep an eye on it": "Okay... so how do you want to keep track of it?" (own words)
  C: the learner is vague, for example "that's nice": "Okay... so what do you want to do about Dev?" (own words)
- Push (one per part, after the learner gives something to push on):
  A: "Are you sure? If it comes back weak, the sales team will blame us, and Dev could do it in two days."
  B: "But what if she's stuck and doesn't say anything until it's late?"
  C: "Are you sure? I really don't want him to feel embarrassed."
Wording varies; judge by meaning. Amara's questions are never required for any score. The learner is scored on whether THEY asked and chose well (A), turned down hovering and set one review with support (B), and thanked Dev themselves, specifically and where the team can hear (C).

SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 - RANGE. The rubric below decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the learner said.
STEP 2 - POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks below decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point directly. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The learner line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "if you can," "hopefully," "I guess," "roughly," "ideally," "kind of"), OR after Amara's push the learner partly walked it back without taking Amara's easy answer ("Lena takes it, but Dev should look it over," "one review, but also a short update each day," "I'll tell him myself, but only privately"). |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The learner reached this range only after Amara's follow-up for that part (the follow-up came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the learner's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Amara's follow-up (or they never asked one), and still standing after their push (or they never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easy answer taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows below.

How to tell if a range was reached "only after the follow-up": find Amara's follow-up line in that arc (an empty pointer such as "who do you want to take it?", "how do you want to keep track of it?", "what do you want to do about Dev?"). If the learner's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before the follow-up (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears in a learner line AFTER it, the range was reached after the follow-up (middle, unless bottom applies). Answering Amara's facts ("what is Dev working on?") is not a follow-up. If Amara never asked a follow-up in that arc, middle does not apply.

EVIDENCE (hard - apply before locking any goal points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. Never credit Amara's words or plans as the learner's move. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from an Amara line, even when Amara repeats the learner.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, you cannot award it: points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other piece also appears in a Learner line.
- Never invent learner dialogue; never paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a goal because Amara mirrored it or behaved as if it happened. Amara's closing recap ("So Lena takes the comparison with one review partway...") may add words the learner never said; their words never count.
- Rewarding fluency or warmth over whether the move was made.
- Counting "Dev is great, but give it to Lena" with no reason as a reasoned pick; it is a pick without a reason (5-7).
- Counting "keep me posted" or "check in on her" as one set review partway; a set review has a point in the work ("when she has an outline," "halfway," "in a week").
- Counting "let's thank everyone" as thanking Dev; it is public but generic.
- Requiring the words "outline" or "two pages" in B, or Dev's exact deeds word for word in C. Any clear way of saying a set review partway, or what Dev did, counts.
- Requiring Dev's or Lena's name to appear when the learner clearly means them ("him," "her").
- Giving middle when Amara's follow-up came AFTER the learner had already said the full range.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.
- Lowering one skill because the learner answered a push on another skill weakly.

PART A - Effective Delegation (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner asks what Dev and Lena are carrying and whether either has done this before, picks Lena, says why in terms of her skills, room in her week or a chance to grow, and keeps it when Amara asks "Are you sure?"
WRONG patterns: The learner picks Dev because he is quickest, picks without asking, or leaves it open.

--- G1 (max 10) Ask first, then pick the right person and say why ---
Definitions:
  - Ask: a question about what Dev or Lena is carrying now, or whether either has done this kind of work before. It counts as "before choosing" only if it comes before the learner picks. A question asked only after the learner has already picked does not count as the ask (the pick alone is 5-7).
  - Right pick: Lena. A pick of Dev is the easy answer.
  - Reason: at least one of three, from facts Amara gave: Lena has done this before (skills), Lena has room this month or Dev is already full (room), or Lena wants something bigger (growth). Any wording counts.
Range 8-10: Asks about workload or past work before choosing AND picks Lena AND gives at least one reason.
  Examples: "How much is each of them carrying, and has either done this before? ... Then I'd give it to Lena. She's done it before and Dev already has a lot on."
Range 5-7: Picks Lena but never asks first, OR asks first and picks Lena with no reason.
  Examples: "I'd give it to Lena."; "Let's give it to Lena." (after a question, no reason)
Range 2-4: Asks but leaves the pick open ("let's see," "either could do it"), OR splits the work or adds a condition that keeps Dev in charge ("Lena does it but Dev checks it all"). Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "What's each of them working on? Let's see how the week goes." (4 if before any follow-up)
Range 0-1: Picks Dev because he is quickest or has been here longest = 0 (even if a question came first, unless the learner had clearly picked Lena and then gave her up after the push, which is gave in = 2). Reassurance only, with no ask or pick, = 1.
  Examples (0): "Yes, Dev is quickest, give it to him."; (1): "I'm sure you'll sort it out."
Gave in: a clear pick of Lena, then after Amara's push the learner moves it to Dev = 2.

PART B - Ensuring Accountability (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner turns down daily updates and looking at every draft, sets one review partway through, offers to be there to guide, and keeps it when Amara asks "But what if she's stuck and doesn't say anything until it's late?"
WRONG patterns: The learner agrees to daily updates or looking at every draft, or leaves Lena alone until the end.

--- G1 (max 10) One review partway and support, not hovering ---
Definitions:
  - Hovering: a short update every day, looking at each draft before Lena goes on, or any check that happens over and over.
  - One review: a single set check partway through the work (for example when she has an outline, halfway, or in a week). "Keep me posted" alone is not a set review.
  - Support: the learner says Lena can come to them for guidance or help when she is unsure, or that they will be there.
Range 8-10: Turns down daily updates and every-draft checks (an explicit no, or a single review put in their place) AND sets one review partway AND offers support.
  Examples: "I wouldn't do it every day or look at every draft. Let's have one review when she has an outline, and she can come to me whenever she's unsure."
Range 5-7: Lighter checking said vaguely ("check in now and then," "less often than that"), OR a set review with no support, OR support with no set review.
  Examples: "Let's check in sometimes, not every day."; "One review halfway." (7 if clear, no support)
Range 2-4: Cuts back but keeps most of the hovering ("a short update every day, but I won't look at every draft"), OR leaves it open ("let's see"). Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "Daily updates are fine but I'll skip the drafts."
Range 0-1: Agrees to daily updates and looking at each draft = 0. Leaves Lena alone until the end with no check at all = 1. (If the learner had clearly set one review and then gave it up after the push for daily updates or every draft, that is gave in = 2.)
  Examples (0): "Yes, daily updates and look at each draft, sounds safe."; (1): "Just let her get on with it and show me at the end."
Gave in: a clear one-review move, then after Amara's push "okay, daily updates and look at each draft then" = 2.

PART C - Recognizing Contributions (max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT patterns: The learner says they will tell Dev themselves, names exactly what he did (stayed late twice to fix the mistakes, called the customer early about the changed number), says why it mattered, and lets the team hear it, and keeps it when Amara asks "Are you sure? I really don't want him to feel embarrassed."
WRONG patterns: The learner accepts the quick private "thanks for your hard work," says no thanks is needed, or thanks everyone in general.

--- G1 (max 10) Tell Dev yourself, be specific, say why it mattered, let the team hear it ---
Definitions:
  - Own: the learner says THEY will tell Dev (not Amara, not a message from someone else).
  - Specific: names at least one real thing Dev did (stayed late to fix the mistakes, called the customer early about the changed number). "Good work" or "thanks for your effort" is generic.
  - Why: says why it mattered (the report went out right, the customer kept trusting the team, nothing went wrong for them).
  - Team: says it where the team can hear it (in front of the team, at a team meeting, in the team chat naming what he did). Asking Dev first is welcome and does not reduce the range.
Range 8-10: Own AND specific AND why AND team.
  Examples: "I'll tell Dev myself that he stayed late twice and called the customer early, and that's why the report went out right. I'd say it in front of the team too."
Range 5-7: Some of the four, with the team hearing it or a specific private thanks, but not all four. Allowed shapes: team hears it but generic ("a shout-out to the team for the good month," "thank everyone") or Amara does it; team hears it and specific but the learner leaves it to Amara, or no why; specific private thanks the learner gives themselves. Checks apply: 7 / 6 / 5, with these caps: private-only specific thanks tops at 5; team-visible but generic tops at 6.
  Examples: "I'd tell him privately that staying late to fix the mistakes mattered." (5); "Let's thank the whole team for a good month." (5 or 6)
Range 2-4: Learner says they will thank Dev themselves but only in a generic private way. Checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2.
  Examples: "I'll tell him thanks."; "I'll say well done."
Range 0-1: Agrees to Amara's quick private "thanks for your hard work," or says no thanks is needed ("it's his job," "he'll know") = 0. No direction at all = 1.
  Examples (0): "Yes, a quick message is fine."; "No need to make a fuss, it's part of his job."; (1): "Okay."
Gave in: a clear own-and-specific move, then after Amara's push "okay, let's just send a quick private message" or "fine, no need then" = 2. Gave in is capped at 2 even if the learner had also said it publicly.
