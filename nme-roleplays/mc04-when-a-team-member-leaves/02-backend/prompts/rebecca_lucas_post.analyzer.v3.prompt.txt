PART A - Building Effective Teams (building_effective_teams, max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT: before Lucas leaves, the manager agrees with the team who will plan the week and who will check the work, plus how the team will work together.
WRONG: "Lucas writes it down and whoever's free picks it up," or nothing specific.

--- G1 (max 10) Cover Lucas's two jobs with clear owners, agreed with the team ---
Pieces:
  - P1 Planning owner: who will plan who works on what each week (a named role, "one person," a pair, or a clear rota with a set order). "Whoever's free" is NOT an owner.
  - P2 Checking owner: who will check work before it goes to clients (same rules).
  - P3 With the team: meets, sits down with, or agrees it with the whole team, or with the team and Lucas together, before Lucas leaves. Deciding alone and announcing it ("I'll tell them who does what") is not P3.
  - P4 How the team works: when the team checks in (a set weekly meeting or quick check), who people go to when they're stuck, or how work moves to the checker. One clear item is enough.
  - A generic "agree who does what" that is not tied to the planning or the checking counts as ONE owner piece only (P1 or P2, not both).
  - Lucas showing the new owners how he does it, or writing notes for them, is welcome, no points.
Range 8-10: P1 + P2 + P3 + P4.
  Examples: "Before Lucas goes, I'll get the whole team together with him and agree who plans each week and who checks the work, and we'll do a quick check-in every Monday so people know who to ask."
Range 5-7: P1 + P2 plus one of (P3, P4). OR one owner piece + P3 + P4.
  Examples: "I'll sit down with the team and Lucas and agree who takes the planning and who does the checking."
Range 2-4: P1 + P2 alone, decided alone. OR one owner piece plus at most one of (P3, P4). Examples: "I'll get the team together and work out who does what." (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Notes or training from Lucas alone, with no owner = 2 (fixed). Only asks questions about the jobs, with no plan = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: "Lucas writes it down and whoever's free picks it up," or agreeing it can all be sorted out later = 0. Generic with nothing specific ("we'll manage," "I'll make sure it's covered") = 1.
Gave in: an owner plan, then after the push "yeah, notes are fine, whoever's free can do it" = 2.

PART B - Managing Stakeholders (managing_stakeholders, max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT: clients have one main person on the team to go to, set up so it works.
WRONG: clients email whoever they like, "reply faster," or "be more careful."

--- G1 (max 10) One main person for clients, set up so it works ---
Pieces:
  - Q1 One main person: one named role or "one person on my team" that clients go to now. A shared team inbox that one person looks after counts. A shared inbox "everyone watches" does not.
  - Q2 Backup: someone who answers when that person is away.
  - Q3 All questions go there: client questions are sent to that person; others pass them on.
  - Q4 Clients are told: Lucas introduces that person on his next client calls, or the manager or Lucas tells clients who to go to now.
  - Q5 Shared list: one place the team writes down what clients asked and what they were told.
Range 8-10: Q1 + at least ONE of (Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5).
  Examples: "One person on the team becomes the main contact for clients, with a backup, and Lucas introduces them on his next calls."
Range 5-7: Q1 alone.
  Examples: "From now on, one person deals with all the clients."
Range 2-4: A shared fix with no single person ("we'll keep a shared list," "we'll check with each other before replying," "a team inbox everyone watches"), OR only tells clients something with no one to go to (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks who has been answering = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: "Let them email whoever they like," "whoever's free answers," or "everyone just reply faster" as the whole answer = 0. Generic with no change named ("be more careful," "better communication," "keep each other updated," "I'll look into it") = 1.
Gave in: one main person, then after the push "fine, let them email whoever" = 2. "Sure, others can help too, but one person still leads" is NOT giving in.

======================================================================
RIGHT: ask both finalists about real things they did before, and compare them the same way.
WRONG: go with Nina because Lucas vouches for her.

--- G1 (max 10) Real past situations, compared the same way ---
Definitions:
  - Real past situations: asking what they actually did before ("tell me about a time…," "what did you do when…," "give me an example of when you caught a mistake / handled an unhappy client / had too much at once").
  - Same way: the same questions for both, or scoring or rating both on the same points or the same sheet.
  - Asking what they did and how it turned out is welcome; it is not needed for 10.
Range 8-10: Real past situations AND compared the same way.
  Examples: "I'll ask Nina and Omar the same questions about real times they caught a mistake or handled an unhappy client, and score both on the same sheet."
Range 5-7: Real past situations but no same-way comparison, OR the same questions or scoring for both but not about real past situations (for example "what would you do if…").
  Examples: "I'll ask each of them about a time they handled too much at once."; "I'll ask both the same questions and see who does best."
Range 2-4: Won't decide on Lucas's word alone but has no way of judging ("I want to interview both properly," "Lucas's view is one input"), OR names the skills that matter with no way to check them (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks what the job involves = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: Goes with Nina on Lucas's word = 0. "I'll chat with them and go with whoever feels right" = 1.
Gave in: an interview plan, then after the push "you're right, let's just take Nina" = 2.

LESSONS (the ONLY manager lessons copy may teach; they come from this course's videos; use the ideas in your own words, never paste these lines)
A · covering Lucas's jobs (video: a team agreeing together who does what)
  - When a job has no clear owner, each person thinks someone else has it. Work gets checked twice, or not at all, and mistakes reach clients.
  - Agree it with the whole team, out loud, before the change. Then everyone knows their job, says yes to it, and knows who does the rest.
  - Doing it while Lucas is still here means he can show people how he does it and answer their questions. Most of what he knows is in his head.
  - Agree how the team works too: when you check in, and who to ask when stuck. Looking at what you agreed again at each check-in keeps everyone on the same page.
  - Notes alone give nobody the job. "Whoever's free" means it is nobody's job.
B · who clients go to (video: ending mix-ups about who answers clients)
  - When anyone can answer, each person thinks it is their job, or thinks someone else will reply. That is how one question got two answers and another waited two days.
  - One main person for clients leads every reply, so clients get one clear answer. A backup covers when that person is away, so nobody waits.
  - All client questions go to that person, and the final answer comes from them.
  - One shared list of what clients asked and what they were told lets anyone see what was said.
  - Looking back at recent client emails shows where the mix-ups happened.
  - Lucas telling clients who their new person is makes the switch easy, because clients already trust him.
C · running the interviews (video: hiring by asking about real past work)
  - A good word from a friend, a nice resume, or a quick feeling about someone does not show how they will do this job.
  - What people really did before is the best sign of what they will do. So ask "tell me about a time when…" for things this job needs: catching a mistake before a client sees it, an unhappy client, too much work at once.
  - Ask more about each answer: what they did, why, and how it turned out.
  - The same questions for both, and scoring both on the same simple sheet right after each interview, is fair and makes them easy to compare. It is fair to Omar too. If Nina is best, the scores will show it.
