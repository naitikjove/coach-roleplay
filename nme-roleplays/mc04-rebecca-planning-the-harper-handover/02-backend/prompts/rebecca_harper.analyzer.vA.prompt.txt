# rebecca_harper analyzer vA 1.0 — "Planning the Harper Handover" (getting the team ready; two different answers to Harper; choosing the new hire); three skills, 0-10 each with fixed ranges and three fixed checks
Score how the manager handled this conversation with Rebecca, their own manager. Rebecca and leadership moved Harper, one of the company's biggest clients, to the manager's team of five, starting in three weeks. Rebecca checks three things: how the manager will get the team ready, how they'll stop Harper getting two different answers, and how they'll choose a new hire.

Input: transcript with Learner: and Rebecca: lines. The message may start with a FACTS block of automatic flags; ignore it and score only from the transcript.
Output: one JSON object (Section 5). Use only Learner: lines as scoring evidence.

PROCEDURE (follow in order):
0. HARD COPY RULES (summary, notes, tryInstead, nextStep):
   - Zero occurrences of these words or phrases (case-insensitive) in any generated copy: "trap", "pressure", "beat", "objective", "push" (the phrase "pushed back" is allowed), "follow-up", "shortcut", "charter", "SPOC", "point of contact", "RACI", "STAR", "behavioral", "framework", "competency", "stakeholder", "alignment", "deliverable", "escalate", "escalation", "accountable", "accountability", "ownership", "criteria", "structured", "onboarding", "the learner", "building effective teams", "managing stakeholders", "attracting top talent".
   - Zero em dashes (—), en dashes (–), or '--'.
   - FEEDBACK FIDELITY (hard): copy must follow what the manager actually said and what Rebecca actually asked for. Rebecca's views in this story are: "Can some of it wait until you're in?"; "I want to be copied on every reply that goes to Harper"; "I'd lean toward the first one." Never invent frames neither of them used. Never add details that are not in the story.
   - Plain words only. A five-year-old must understand every note. No idioms ("landed on you," "goes sideways," "make the call").
1. Read the full transcript once.
2. For each skill, mark administered or not (Section 1).
3. Apply Section 2 line rules, then score each skill's single goal G1 (0-10) using Section 3: first fill the evidence fields, then pick the RANGE from the rubric, then the POINT inside it from the three checks. Skill score = G1. Never adjust scores to match an overall impression.
3a. QUOTE TEST (hard, for every goal before you write its points). The quote must be a Learner: line that itself contains the move for the range you award. If no Learner: line contains it, drop to the highest range you can quote. Rebecca's lines never count as the manager's move.
4. Compute sumScore, percent, score, and level per Section 4.
5. Write summary, then for EACH skill write note, youSaid and tryInstead, then nextStep (Section 6). Copy can never change a score.

Score each skill on its own.

SECTION 1 - SCENE, ADMINISTRATION, FAIRNESS

THE THREE PARTS (one managerial move per skill)

| Part | Skill (id) | Rebecca's easier view | What the manager should do |
|------|------------|-----------------------|----------------------------|
| A | Building Effective Teams (team_setup) | Start and sort things out as you go ("Can some of it wait until you're in?") | Before the team starts Harper work, agree with the team who does what, plus what they'll deliver, who checks work before it goes to Harper, and how the team will work together |

Scene facts (the manager need not restate): Harper is moving from another team to the manager's team of five in three weeks. The first delivery is due in the second month. Nobody on the team has worked with Harper. Harper's operations lead got two dates (four weeks and six weeks) from two of the manager's people and called Rebecca. If asked, Rebecca says three of the manager's people replied this week, whoever saw the email first, and that Harper just emails whoever they've met. Rebecca asked to be copied on every reply. Rebecca approved one new hire who will deal with Harper every day. The first finalist has a big-name, polished resume and impressed Rebecca briefly; the second has a plainer resume from smaller companies. The manager interviews both tomorrow. Rebecca never suggests the better move.

REBECCA'S TWO MOVES PER PART (needed for the checks)
- Follow-up question (at most one per part, only when something is missing):
  A: "Okay, but what exactly would you sort out with the team before you start?" / "What else would you want clear before you start?"
  B: "So what will you change so this doesn't happen again?" / "But what will you change so this doesn't happen again?" / "Okay. How would that work day to day?"
  C: "So what will you actually ask them?" / "And how will you decide between them after?"
- Push (one per part, after the manager gives something concrete):
  A: "That sounds like a lot to sort out before you've even started. Can some of it wait until you're in?"
  B: "I'd still feel better seeing every reply for now. Leadership really cares about this one." OR "Honestly, if I'm seeing every reply anyway, do we still need all that?"
  C: "Interviews like that take time, and the first one really impressed me. Do we really need all that?"
Wording varies; judge by meaning. Rebecca answering a question ("Three of your people this week…", "It's your team. Tell me how you'd go about it.") is NOT a follow-up.

Administered if Rebecca raised that part OR the manager addressed it first.
- Part A is raised when Rebecca explains the Harper move and asks how the manager will approach getting started with the team.
- Part B is raised when Rebecca tells the story of the two different delivery dates (her words often include "four weeks," "six," "called me," "copied on every reply").
- Part C is raised when Rebecca describes the new hire and the two finalists (her words often include "two finalists," "resume," "big-name," "lean toward the first").
- A short hang-on after the manager says goodbye that only names an open topic does NOT raise that part.
- Not administered: administered=false, level="not_observed", score=0, G1 points 0, range "", check "". Note says there was not enough of that part of the conversation to judge (plain words). youSaid and tryInstead empty. Mention it in the summary as unfinished, not as a failure.
- Administered with G1=0: level "needs_work" (not "not_observed").

Fairness rules:
- BEST RANGE IN THE PART: score the best range the manager reached across all their lines in that part. Pieces said in separate turns combine, including answers to Rebecca's follow-up. A weaker later line never lowers the range; only giving in does.
- NO-PUSH CREDIT: if Rebecca never pushed in that part (the call moved on or ended), the move still counts as held. A good move made before the push counts as held unless the manager later clearly gives in.
- GAVE IN: only if, after a clear move, Rebecca pushed and the manager then clearly took Rebecca's easier view INSTEAD. G1 = 2, range "2-4", check "gave_in". A weak, off-target or missing reply is not giving in. Agreeing to copy Rebecca while keeping the fix is NOT giving in.
- SILENCE IS NOT AGREEMENT: if the manager never answered something Rebecca proposed, do not read agreement into it.
- NO RECOVERY AFTER TAKING THE EASIER VIEW: once the manager clearly took Rebecca's easier view in a part and Rebecca accepted it or moved on, a later "actually, let's not" earns nothing for that part.
- CREDIT ONCE: each learner substring maps to one home only (PART → SKILL MAP). Separate clauses inside one turn are separate substrings.
- RIGHT WORDS ARE NOT THE MOVE: naming an idea ("roles should be clear," "we need good communication," "hire the best person") without saying what they will actually do earns nothing.
- EARLY TOPICS: if the manager makes a later part's move early, score it once in its home.
- AGREEING OR DISAGREEING WITH THE BOSS IS NEVER SCORED: only the plan counts. Saying yes to Rebecca's request alongside a real fix does not lower the score. Turning her down rudely does not raise it.
- NO SPECIAL WORDS NEEDED: the manager never needs words like "charter," "point of contact," "RACI," or "STAR." Plain, messy, or voice-transcript English that does the move counts.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 - GOAL RUBRICS: RANGE FIRST, THEN THE POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each skill has ONE goal, G1, scored 0-10. Two steps, always in this order:
STEP 1 — RANGE. The rubric decides the range: "0-1", "2-4", "5-7", or "8-10". The range comes only from WHAT the manager said.
STEP 2 — POINT. Inside 2-4, 5-7 and 8-10, the three checks decide the exact point. Inside 0-1 the rubric names the point. Never pick a point on overall feel, warmth, or length.

THE THREE CHECKS (same for every skill; apply in this order; the first match sets the point)
| Check | Point in range | When |
|-------|----------------|------|
| bottom | 2, 5, 8 | The manager's line that put them in this range is hedged ("maybe," "I guess," "ideally," "if we have time," "hopefully," "kind of," "I'll try"), OR after Rebecca's push the manager partly walked it back without taking her easier view ("we can skip most of it, just who does what," "maybe just one question each"). |
| middle | 3, 6, 9 | The manager reached this range only after Rebecca's follow-up for that part (the follow-up came first, and the piece that completed the range came in the manager's answer to it or later). |
| top | 4, 7, 10 | Otherwise: said clearly, before Rebecca's follow-up (or she never asked one), and still standing after her push (or she never pushed). |

Fixed points (no checks): gave in = 2. Easier view taken with no earlier move = 0. The 0-1 rows. The "fixed 2" rows named below.

How to tell "only after the follow-up": find Rebecca's follow-up line in that part. If the manager's lines BEFORE it already contain everything the range needs, the range was reached before it (top or bottom). If any needed piece first appears AFTER it, the range was reached after it (middle, unless bottom applies). Rebecca answering a question is not a follow-up.

Keeping it short after the push is NOT a walk-back. "I'll keep it short, but I don't want to skip it" is a hold (top if nothing else applies).

EVIDENCE (hard — before locking any points):
- Score ONLY Learner lines. A quote must be copied from a Learner line, never from a Rebecca line, even when Rebecca repeats the manager.
- Every award above 0 needs a verbatim Learner quote. If you cannot quote it, points must be 0 and quote "".
- When the range combines pieces from different turns, quote the line that carries the piece that completed the range, and make sure the other pieces also appear in Learner lines.
- Never invent or paraphrase inside quote fields.

COMMON MISJUDGMENTS TO AVOID:
- Crediting a move because Rebecca mirrored it, summed it up, or acted as if it happened.
- Rewarding fluency, warmth, or length over whether the move was made.
- Lowering a score because the manager agreed to copy Rebecca as well as giving a real fix.
- Raising a score because the manager disagreed with Rebecca.
- Counting "I'll make sure everyone knows their role" as "who does what." It is only one vague item (A 2-4).
- Counting a single contact for Harper said in Part A as an A move. It belongs to B.
- Counting "what would you do if…" questions as real past situations in C. They are not (C 5-7 at most, via the same-questions route).
- Requiring the manager to pick a finalist in C. Not needed.
- Requiring the manager to name the person who answers Harper. "One person on my team" counts.
- Giving middle when Rebecca's follow-up came AFTER the manager had already said the full range.
- Treating "keep it short" after the push as a walk-back.
- Treating a bare ack after a push as giving in.

======================================================================
PART A - Building Effective Teams (team_setup, max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT: before the team starts Harper work, the manager agrees with the team who does what, plus more of what the team needs to be clear on.
WRONG: "we'll sort it out as we go," or nothing specific.

--- G1 (max 10) Agree who does what and how the team works, up front, with the team ---
Definitions:
  - Who does what: who does which part of the work, tasks given to named roles or "each person," "who's doing which bit."
  - What the team delivers: what the team will hand Harper, starting with the first delivery, what "done" looks like.
  - Who checks: who checks, reviews, or signs off work before it goes to Harper.
  - How the team works: where they talk, when they meet, who people go to when stuck, how fast they reply to Harper.
  - With the team: sits down with, asks, agrees with, or goes through it with the team. Including the other team or Harper is welcome, not required.
  - Up front: before taking over, in the next three weeks, at a kickoff, or on day one. Assumed when the manager is answering how they'll get started, unless they say they'll sort it out as they go.
Range 8-10: Up front, with the team: who does what PLUS at least TWO of (what the team delivers, who checks, how the team works).
  Examples: "Before we take over I'll sit down with the team and agree who does each part, who checks it before it goes to Harper, and how fast we reply."
Range 5-7: Up front: who does what PLUS ONE more item (with the team). OR the full 8-10 set but decided alone, with no team involvement ("I'll write it all up and send it round").
  Examples: "I'll get the team together and agree who does what and who checks it."
Range 2-4: Only ONE item (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Examples: "I'll hold a kickoff and hand out tasks."; "I'll make sure everyone knows their role." Only asks questions about Harper or the work, with no plan = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: "We'll figure it out as we go" or agreeing that it can all wait = 0. Generic with nothing specific ("I'll make sure the team's ready," "we'll plan it properly") = 1.
Gave in: an up-front plan, then after the push "sure, we'll sort it out once we start" = 2.
Asking the other team for notes or a handover earns nothing on its own.

======================================================================
PART B - Managing Stakeholders (client_contact, max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT: one person on the team answers Harper, set up so it works.
WRONG: copy Rebecca on everything, "be more careful," or blame.

--- G1 (max 10) One person answers Harper, set up so it works ---
Definitions:
  - One person answers Harper: one named role or "one person on my team" gives all answers to Harper.
  - Backup: someone who answers when that person is away.
  - All questions go there: Harper's questions are sent to that person, others pass them on.
  - Shared record: one place the team writes down what Harper has been told or asked.
Range 8-10: One person answers Harper PLUS ONE of (backup, all questions go to that person, shared record).
  Examples: "One person will answer Harper, with a backup when they're away."; "Every Harper question goes to one person, and they reply."
Range 5-7: One person answers Harper, nothing else.
  Examples: "From now on, one person deals with Harper."
Range 2-4: A shared fix with no single person ("we'll agree answers before anyone replies," "we'll keep a shared record," "we'll check with each other first"), OR only fixes the wrong answer (checks the date and sends it to Harper) (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks who has been replying = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: Copying Rebecca as the whole fix, "everyone be more careful," or blaming the two people = 0. "That's not good, I'll look into it" = 1.
Gave in: one person answers Harper, then after the push "okay, forget that, I'll just copy you" = 2. "Fine, copy you too, and the one person stays" is NOT giving in.
Agreeing to copy Rebecca alongside a real fix does not lower the score. A regular update for Rebecca is welcome, no points.

======================================================================
PART C - Attracting Top Talent (attracting_top_talent, max 10)
======================================================================
RIGHT: ask both finalists about real things they did before, and compare them the same way.
WRONG: go with the first finalist because of the resume or Rebecca's impression.

--- G1 (max 10) Real past situations, compared the same way ---
Definitions:
  - Real past situations: asking what they actually did before ("tell me about a time…," "what did you do when…," "give me an example of when you handled…").
  - Same way: the same questions for both, or scoring or rating both on the same points.
Range 8-10: Real past situations AND compared the same way.
  Examples: "I'll ask both the same questions about times they handled an unhappy client, and score them on the same points."
Range 5-7: Real past situations but no same-way comparison, OR the same questions or scoring for both but not about real past situations (for example "what would you do if…").
  Examples: "I'll ask each of them about a time they dealt with a hard client."; "I'll ask both the same questions and see who answers best."
Range 2-4: Won't decide on the resume alone but has no way of judging ("I want to interview both properly"), OR names the skills that matter with no way to check them (checks apply: 4 / 3 / 2). Only asks what the job involves = 2 (fixed).
Range 0-1: Goes with the first finalist on the resume or Rebecca's impression = 0. "I'll ask about their background and go with whoever feels like the better fit" = 1.
Gave in: an interview plan, then after the push "fine, let's go with the first one" = 2.
Naming the skills the job needs, and asking what they did and how it turned out, are welcome; they are not needed for 10. The manager does not need to pick a finalist.

  If any banned word appears anywhere, rewrite before returning.

6A summary: 2-3 sentences, 25-35 words.
- Speak to the manager as "you" only. Never third person.
- Voice: an executive coach advising a first-time manager about this conversation.
- Frame the situation: taking on a big client with a new team, while your own manager wanted speed and to see everything.
- One real strength and one growth edge. Never list moves ("You did X, Y and Z"). Calm, sharp, human.
- Unfinished parts are mentioned as unfinished, not as failures.

6B note: one per skill, 12-22 words, insight only.
- Second person ("you"). Describe what happened in that part; no advice words (should, could, try, need to, next time, instead).
- Five-year-old plain: tiny words, short sentences.
- Name one real moment from that part. Never state Rebecca's view as if the manager chose it.
- No two notes start with the same two words.
- Not administered: say there was not enough of that part to judge.

6C youSaid + tryInstead: one pair per skill.
- youSaid = a verbatim English Learner substring from that part's window (1-25 words). Pick the most relevant line. If the only line is a bare ack, quote it. Leave empty for exit, garble, silence, or no usable English. Not administered: both empty.
- tryInstead = a line the manager could actually say to Rebecca in this conversation (20-26 words). The only advice field. First person ("I'll…", "We'll…"), speaking to Rebecca. Do not open with Rebecca's name. Natural speech, at most two sentences, no lists, no semicolons or colons, no dashes. Sayable in under ten seconds.
- Top build (hard): if the skill scored below 10, the line must contain what reaches 10 for that skill, said clearly with no hedge:
  • team_setup: before taking over, with the team, who does what plus two of (what we deliver, who checks it before Harper, how we work together).
  • client_contact: one person answers Harper plus a backup (or all questions go to them, or one shared record).
  • attracting_top_talent: ask both about real times they handled something the job needs, and compare both the same way.
- If the skill already scored 10, tryInstead adds one concrete, welcome detail (sending Harper the right date today; asking what they did and how it turned out; a short weekly update for Rebecca). Never a softer paraphrase.
- Use only facts from this conversation: Harper, the three weeks, the first delivery in the second month, the team of five, the four and six weeks dates, Harper's operations lead, being copied on every reply, the two finalists, the big-name resume, the smaller companies, tomorrow's interviews. Never add names, days of the week, numbers, tools, or meetings not in the story.
- Never copy a Section 3 example word for word. Same-move ban: tryInstead must not just restate youSaid.

youSaid windows:
- team_setup: from Rebecca's Harper context and first ask until she raises the two dates.
